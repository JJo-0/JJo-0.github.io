Transforming memory, reordering space, and creaturely praise

This application presents the supplied literary, rhetorical, discourse, and metaphor-critical study interactively. Beyond private encouragement to forget the past and start anew, it examines prophetic poetics that pause Israel’s exodus memory and reorient perception toward emerging new creation. Audit of poetic structure and lexical progression

The source finds insufficient lexical support for a mechanical chiasm here, preferring **ripple-like progression** from divine declaration to the wider creation and community. This differs from the structural preference in the first report.

Hebrew lexical cohesion and plausibility by structural model

Linear progression

18 (Memory interrupted) → 19a (Attention) → 19b–20a (Space / creatures) → 20b–21 (Purposeful communal praise). The purpose expressions לְהַשְׁקוֹת and יְסַפֵּרוּ support progression.

Epexegetical expansion

The way / rivers of verse 19 recur as water / rivers in 20, adding creaturely honor and the chosen people’s drink in expanding poetic elaboration.

C, D & E. Memory rhetoric and temporal / aspect map

The prohibitions אַל־תִּזְכְּרוּ / אַל־תִּתְבֹּנָנוּ rhetorically suspend foundational exodus memory. Hebrew aspect and prophetic presentification connect remembered past, emerging present, and eschatological future.

N, Q & Divine Agency: The reversal after 43:22 and close commentary

The covenant lawsuit in 22–28 follows the saving proclamation of 18–21, locating salvation in prior grace and verse 25’s forgiveness rather than merit.

Testing the order: Divine initiative and human response

Some preaching makes clearing one’s past a condition for divine new action. Grammatically, verse 18 is not a conditional clause governing verse 19; grace precedes response.

Initiative by grammatical subject

Divine creative actions

עֹשֶׂה (Do), אָשִׂים (Place), נָתַתִּי (Give), בְחִירִי (My chosen), יָצַרְתִּי (Form)

Creaturely / communal responses

אַל־תִּזְכְּרוּ (Suspend remembrance), תֵדָעוּהָ (Recognize), לְהַשְׁקוֹת (Give drink), תְּכַבְּדֵנִי (Honor), יְסַפֵּרוּ (Recount)

* Conclusion: Salvation is not a reward following a qualifying decision; prior divine proclamation reshapes perception.

Isaiah 43:16–28: Close-reading explorer

Key Hebrew: הַנּוֹתֵן בַּיָּם דֶּרֶךְ (The one making a way in the sea)

Rhetorical function: Calls Israel’s identity-forming exodus victory into the audience’s attention.

Key Hebrew: אַל־תִּזְכְּרוּ רִאשֹׁנוֹת (Do not remember former things)

Rhetorical function: Interrupts fixation on the just-recalled exodus so an inherited pattern does not obscure new deliverance.

Key Hebrew: הִנְנִי עֹשֶׂה חֲדָשָׁה עַתָּה תִצְמָח (Behold, I am doing a new thing; now it sprouts)

Rhetorical function: Way and river metaphors reorder death-marked space as a lifeline; creatures’ praise anticipates the people’s benefit.

Key Hebrew: עַם־זוּ יָצַרְתִּי לִי תְּהִלָּתִי יְסַפֵּרוּ (The people formed for myself will recount my praise)

Rhetorical function: Re-created Israel’s vocation is worship expressed as public testimony among nations, beyond private well-being.

Key Hebrew: וְלֹא־אֹתִי קָרָאתָ יַעֲקֹב (Yet you have not called on me, Jacob)

Rhetorical function: The reversal after verse 21 indicts failed worship, making salvation depend on verse 25’s divine forgiveness rather than the people’s merit.

R & S. Final literary-theological synthesis and SBL references

Isaiah 43:18–21 offers neither private optimism nor forced amnesia. It temporarily suspends foundational exodus memory and redirects attention toward God’s emerging new creation. The one who dried the sea now creates rivers in dry terrain, transforming the pattern of deliverance. Creatures formerly marking cursed ruins become praising recipients and anticipate the people’s response. Sovereign election and formation arise from grace, with the aim of public testimony rather than survival alone. The following lawsuit exposes failure and makes this community depend on forgiveness rather than religious merit. The passage thus enlarges the horizon of divine saving power, raising a past-bound community toward a renewed eschatological vocation.
