Scholarship & Research Trends

Scholarly consensus map & ten major Acts researchers

The supplied study distinguishes strong agreement, majority positions, and contested questions, and presents selected scholars’ theses. These consensus labels are its assessments.

1. Strong consensus (Source assessment)

Luke and Acts share an author and form a two-volume Hellenistic historical project. Acts and Paul’s letters differ in theological nuances concerning Torah and journeys. Jewish hostility in the text belongs to first-century intra-Jewish conflict and cannot justify later antisemitism.

2. Significant majority (Source assessment)

The “parting of the ways” was gradual, continuing into the third and fourth centuries rather than occurring at one stroke in 70 CE. The source favors composition in the 80s–90s.

3. Highly contested questions

The nature of Codex Bezae’s longer readings—scribal elaboration or an early edition—and the precise theological referent of David’s tent in Acts 15.

Three expanding areas of Acts research

1. Paul’s Jewishness Challenges to a first-century rupture model: scholars including Matthew Thiessen emphasize Paul’s continuing Jewish identity and Torah-related practices.
2. Empire criticism Political-theological study of how Christian language adopts and subverts Roman imperial ideology, including Jesus as Lord in relation to Caesar.
3. Migration & mobility Social and anthropological spatial research on diaspora, refugees, and itinerant networks transmitting material culture and ideas around the Mediterranean.

Single Page Overview Reference

One-page Acts map

A reference grid summarizing historical setting, authorship, structure, five central themes, and five historical-theological questions.

Historical Setting

The first-century Greco-Roman world: Pax Romana; diverse Second Temple Judaism, including Pharisees, Sadducees, diaspora Jews, and God-fearers; diaspora synagogue networks.

Authorship & Structure

The same author as Luke’s Gospel, traditionally identified as Luke the physician.
Three-stage structure: 1) Jerusalem (Chapters 1–7); 2) Judea and Samaria (8–12); 3) The earth’s ends / Rome (13–28).

5 Core Themes
  • The Spirit’s sovereign initiative
  • Mission advancing through persecution
  • Jewish–Gentile barriers crossed
  • The advance of God’s word
  • Spiritual victory over imperial oppression
5 Theological Questions
  • Justification or the Spirit’s power?
  • Replacement or expansion of the Old Testament covenant?
  • Are miracles unique or continuing?
  • Teaching authority: Peter or James?
  • Is sharing possessions obligatory?
