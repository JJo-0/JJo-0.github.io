## 1. Treatment intensity and patient outcomes

Is a stronger drug always better at eliminating cancer? The intuition overlooks normal cells and immune function. Infections or bleeding can make it difficult to continue treatment. The intensity of a regimen and the ultimate benefit to a patient are separate questions.

PARADIGM, published on September 2, 2026, investigated that question in acute myeloid leukemia (AML). People eligible for intensive induction chemotherapy were randomly assigned to azacitidine plus venetoclax or induction chemotherapy. Investigators tracked time to events including treatment failure, relapse and death. <a href="#news-ref-1" data-news-citation="1" aria-label="Go to reference 1" data-astro-reload>[1]</a>

The central numbers are median event-free survival of 14.5 and 6.2 months. Their difference cannot be read as 8.3 extra months of life for every patient. <strong>What counted as an event, who was eligible for comparison, and how was incomplete follow-up handled?</strong> Each question contributes to the meaning of these results.

## 2. Start with what happens in the bone marrow

Bone marrow makes blood cells. Red cells carry oxygen, white cells respond to infection, and platelets help stop bleeding. During normal blood production immature cells pass through several stages of differentiation and maturation before carrying out these roles.

In AML, abnormal immature myeloid cells proliferate and disrupt normal blood-cell production and function. A high white-cell count alone does not describe the disease. Anemia, vulnerability to infection and bleeding all matter. <a href="#news-ref-4" data-news-citation="4" aria-label="Go to reference 4" data-astro-reload>[4]</a>

One early treatment goal is remission. Signs of disease fall below defined criteria and normal blood function recovers. Residual cells can remain even when detectable disease has fallen sharply, and relapse can occur later. Remission therefore does not guarantee a cure.

Both the initial decrease in leukemia cells and the duration of the response matter. The remission rate, time to relapse, overall survival and treatment toxicity measure different aspects of treatment. Improvement in one does not guarantee improvement in the others.

## 3. How do the two strategies act on leukemia?

Intensive induction chemotherapy aims to reduce the leukemia burden substantially in the initial phase and induce remission. PARADIGM allowed standard induction regimens selected under trial criteria. “Induction” describes the phase and objective of treatment. It does not identify one fixed drug. [<a href="#news-ref-1" data-news-citation="1" aria-label="Go to reference 1" data-astro-reload>1</a>, <a href="#news-ref-2" data-news-citation="2" aria-label="Go to reference 2" data-astro-reload>2</a>]

Venetoclax inhibits BCL-2, a protein that helps cells avoid programmed cell death, or apoptosis. Weakening that defense may matter when cancer cells rely on it. Describing the drug only as slowing proliferation misses its target's role in survival.

Azacitidine is a hypomethylating agent. Metabolism within the cell and effects on nucleic acids interact. A single switch does not turn a leukemia cell back into a normal cell. The rationale for the combination is that different actions together may weaken leukemia-cell survival.

Earlier mechanistic work on this combination also examined energy metabolism and oxidative phosphorylation in leukemia stem cells. <a href="#news-ref-3" data-news-citation="3" aria-label="Go to reference 3" data-astro-reload>[3]</a> Here “stem cell” refers to a population functionally associated with maintaining or regenerating disease, rather than the largest cancer cell. The 2018 study cannot explain the cause of every individual outcome in the 2026 PARADIGM trial. <strong>The biological work explains why an effect might be possible. Randomization tests what happened when the treatment strategies were compared.</strong>

## 4. Who enrolled, and how were they compared?

PARADIGM was a multicenter randomized phase 2 trial enrolling 172 adults with newly diagnosed AML considered eligible for intensive treatment. Each arm received 86 participants. Overall median age was 64, and about 72% were in the adverse-risk group under 2022 ELN criteria. <a href="#news-ref-1" data-news-citation="1" aria-label="Go to reference 1" data-astro-reload>[1]</a>

Why randomize? If only healthier patients received one regimen, baseline health could explain differences in outcomes. Randomization reduces that confounding. With 86 in each arm, age, genetic characteristics and comorbidities can still show imbalances by chance.

Eligibility matters. The trial excluded certain genetic subtypes and molecular markers or set additional age conditions. FLT3-mutated and core-binding-factor AML were among the exclusions, and NPM1-mutated AML had an age condition. <a href="#news-ref-2" data-news-citation="2" aria-label="Go to reference 2" data-astro-reload>[2]</a> A shared AML diagnosis alone is insufficient to apply the findings to every patient.

Patients and clinicians knew treatment assignments in this open-label trial. Death is relatively unambiguous, while determining an event linked to changing treatment depends on detailed rules. Randomization strengthens the comparison, but interpretation still requires those definitions.

## 5. What counts as an event in event-free survival?

Event-free survival (EFS) measures time spent without a prespecified event. The registry gives rules involving progression, a treatment switch for persistent leukemia, relapse after remission and death. <a href="#news-ref-2" data-news-citation="2" aria-label="Go to reference 2" data-astro-reload>[2]</a> Event definitions vary across trials and should be checked before comparing EFS.

Overall survival (OS) counts death as the event. A person can still be alive when a treatment failure or relapse counts as an EFS event. An EFS improvement cannot automatically be reported as an OS improvement of the same magnitude.

EFS is a composite endpoint. Grouping treatment failure, relapse and death captures a wider range of events. How much each event contributed to the difference deserves separate attention. Their importance to patients may also differ.

<strong>Interpret an endpoint against the question it answers.</strong> EFS captures persistence and failure of a treatment strategy more broadly than a single initial response rate. Overall survival, quality of life and the effects of subsequent treatment require their own data.

Each point is an arm’s median EFS. Horizontal lines give 95% confidence intervals around the medians. Individual survival ranges and actual Kaplan–Meier curves cannot be inferred from these intervals." source="https://www.nejm.org/doi/abs/10.1056/NEJMoa2602804" credit="Original visualization of summary estimates reported by Fathi et al. at a median follow-up of 21.9 months." />

The horizontal axis is in months. The two points show the longer median EFS in the combination group. The bars express uncertainty in the group median estimate. They do not show where most individual patients fell.

The median relates to when the estimated probability of remaining event-free reaches 0.5. It is distinct from the mean. A median of 14.5 months cannot guarantee any individual duration. Some people experience events earlier, and some remain event-free when observation ends.

The difference between medians is 8.3 months. It compares two summaries of group distributions. It cannot directly compare what would have happened to the same person under both treatments or state how much extra time every person received.

Medians and confidence intervals also reveal little about the whole curve. Knowing whether curves separated early, diverged late or crossed requires time-resolved data. Drawing a plausible-looking curve without those data would invent evidence.

## 7. Figure 2: how does the analysis handle incomplete observation?

<ResearchFigure src="/assets/posts/sep05-deep/aml-km-teaching-example.png" alt="Kaplan–Meier example for five hypothetical patients. Events at times 2, 6 and 8 lower the curve. A censoring mark at time 4 leaves its height unchanged." caption="Figure 2. A hypothetical example illustrates survival analysis. Steps descend at events. When observation ends without an event, censoring changes the risk set for future calculations." source="https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04801797" credit="Original calculation and plot using synthetic teaching data, separate from PARADIGM patient results." />

Suppose five people are followed. At time 2, one has an event, leaving four of five event-free. Estimated survival is 4/5, or 0.8. At time 4, another person's follow-up ends without an event. Their departure is censoring.

At time 6, one of the three still at risk has an event. The new estimate is 0.8 times 2/3, or 8/15. At time 8, one of two remaining people has an event. Multiply by 1/2 to obtain 4/15.

The Kaplan–Meier estimator generalizes this calculation.

<Math display tex={String.raw`\widehat S(t)=\prod_{t_i\le t}\left(1-\frac{d_i}{n_i}\right).`} label="Kaplan–Meier estimate as the product of conditional event-free fractions at event times" />

Here n_i counts the risk set just before an event time, and d_i counts events at that time. The estimate can differ from simply subtracting events from the initial cohort because the relevant risk set is updated after follow-up ends for individuals.

The method assumes a relationship between censoring and event risk. If people systematically leave follow-up as their condition deteriorates, treating the departure as a simple observation ending can affect the estimate. Risk sets shrink toward the right-hand tail, making the numbers at risk and confidence intervals essential companions to the line.

## 8. What a hazard ratio of 0.57 measures

The paper reports an EFS hazard ratio (HR) of 0.57 (95% CI 0.39–0.84), with P = 0.002 in the stratified log-rank test. <a href="#news-ref-1" data-news-citation="1" aria-label="Go to reference 1" data-astro-reload>[1]</a> The HR differs from dividing 6.2 by 14.5 or dividing two event probabilities at a selected time.

<Math display tex={String.raw`h(t)=\lim_{\Delta t\to0}\frac{P(t\le T<t+\Delta t\mid T\ge t)}{\Delta t},\qquad \operatorname{HR}(t)=\frac{h_{\rm AZA+VEN}(t)}{h_{\rm induction}(t)}.`} label="Hazard ratio comparing instantaneous event hazards among those still event-free at that time" />

Conditional on remaining event-free through t, the hazard h(t) describes the event rate in the next short interval. Its units are inverse time. The hazard ratio is dimensionless. A single HR summary depends on assumptions about how hazards relate over time.

In this analysis an HR of 0.57 points toward a lower instantaneous EFS-event hazard in the combination group. It cannot establish that 43% of patients were cured or that the probability of death fell by 43% at every time point. EFS includes events other than death.

P = 0.002 concerns how extreme these data or more extreme results would be under the test's null hypothesis and assumptions. It does not assign a 99.8% probability to the new therapy being correct. The HR, medians and uncertainty describe effect size. Other outcomes help assess benefits and burdens that patients experience.

The horizontal axis gives the proportion of patients. The upper comparison is grade ≥3 infection and the lower comparison grade ≥3 bleeding. Infection was reported in 28% with the combination and 41% with induction. Bleeding was reported in 2% and 12%, respectively. <a href="#news-ref-1" data-news-citation="1" aria-label="Go to reference 1" data-astro-reload>[1]</a> The grade threshold matters because these numbers do not represent every mild infection or bleed.

The absolute infection difference is 13 percentage points. A 13% relative reduction would describe a different calculation, and the relative change depends on the chosen denominator. Start with the two observed proportions and their absolute difference.

The two rows cannot be added to calculate an overall adverse-event rate. One person can experience both infection and bleeding. Overall safety also depends on other adverse events, time exposed to treatment, hospital and transfusion burden, and discontinuations.

Visual overlap of individual group confidence intervals is not a formal test of the difference. The calculations for a between-group comparison and for the range around each proportion differ. The figure displays direction and magnitude without substituting an unreported statistical test.

## 10. The question this trial changes

PARADIGM directly tested an alternative strategy in people eligible for intensive treatment. Its endpoints compare the strategies on EFS and adverse events in a specified population. <strong>The investigators tested the assumption that a more intensive initial attack necessarily yields a better patient outcome.</strong>

One randomized phase 2 trial cannot determine treatment choice for every person with AML. Sample size, eligibility, molecular subtype, follow-up, subsequent therapy and transplantation, OS and quality of life all matter. An individual decision belongs with the clinical team assessing that person.

The three figures serve distinct purposes. Figures 1 and 3 replot publicly reported clinical summaries. Figure 2 uses hypothetical data to explain censoring and survival estimation. Keeping them distinct prevents a teaching illustration from acquiring the status of a clinical observation.

## Which cells expand in leukemia?

The marrow makes blood cells. In normal hematopoiesis, stem-cell descendants mature in stages into oxygen-carrying red cells, infection-fighting white cells and platelets that prevent bleeding. In acute myeloid leukemia (AML), abnormal immature myeloid cells proliferate and interfere with normal production. Although abnormal cells may be abundant, defense against infection may deteriorate, and anemia and bleeding can occur. [National Cancer Institute overview of AML](https://www.cancer.gov/types/leukemia/patient/adult-aml-treatment-pdq)

Treatment aims to reduce leukemia while allowing healthy marrow function to return, manage the risk of relapse and limit injury from therapy itself. Feasibility of a given intensity depends on general health, coexisting disease, leukemia genetics and subsequent options as well as age.

## How do the strategies target cells differently?

Intensive induction chemotherapy aims to reduce the leukemia burden enough to induce remission. Remission means disease signs have fallen substantially by specified criteria. It need not mean that every leukemia cell has disappeared. Read the specific comparator drugs and subsequent treatment in the trial protocol, since “induction chemotherapy” can cover more than one regimen.

Venetoclax targets BCL-2, a protein that inhibits apoptosis. Cells have pathways that respond to damage by undergoing programmed death. When cancer cells depend on BCL-2 to avoid this process, inhibition can lower that resistance. Different AML cells may rely on more than BCL-2. [Original BCL-2 inhibitor study](https://doi.org/10.1038/nm.3048)

Azacitidine is a hypomethylating agent. It affects DNA methylation involved in gene regulation and has RNA-related actions. Combining the drugs pairs different mechanisms rather than simply doubling one type of toxicity. Whether that combination improves patient outcomes requires randomized testing.

### VIALE-A and PARADIGM enrolled different patients

In 2020 VIALE-A tested adding venetoclax to azacitidine in previously untreated AML patients who were ineligible for intensive induction. That result could not settle which regimen was better for people able to receive intensive treatment. Eligibility and the comparator changed in PARADIGM. Lining up median survival values from separate trials cannot replace a direct comparison. [VIALE-A paper](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2012971)

## Different ways of assessing a response

| Assessment | What it measures | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Blood tests | Counts and recovery of normal blood cells | Counts alone cannot rule out all residual leukemia. |
| Marrow morphology | Cell shapes and proportions under a microscope | Morphologic remission differs from a negative result on a more sensitive assay. |
| Multiparameter flow cytometry | Combinations of marker proteins on individual cells | Results depend on sample quality, cell count, antibody panel and analytical method. |
| Targeted molecular testing | Selected fusion genes, variants or transcripts | A marker is needed, and interpretation and sensitivity depend on the marker. |
| Sequencing | Residual variant signals | A detected variant need not represent residual leukemia. |
| Clinical follow-up | Events such as relapse, treatment failure or death, and their timing | Interpret with the event definitions, subsequent treatment, censoring and length of follow-up. |

These are ways to assess AML response and residual disease. The table does not establish that PARADIGM performed every test in the same way. Refer to the trial text and protocol for its actual measurements. ### Why does a negative MRD result leave uncertainty?

Measurable residual disease (MRD) testing looks for disease signals with more sensitive methods in someone who appears to be in remission. A negative result means no signal was detected under the sampling and assay conditions. Only a sample is examined, within a finite detection limit.

For a simple sampling example, suppose the fraction of abnormal cells is p and N cells are sampled independently. The probability of seeing none is given below.

<Math display tex={String.raw`P(\text{zero cells detected})=(1-p)^N\approx e^{-Np}`} label="Probability of missing a low-frequency cell population under independent sampling" />

If p is one in 100,000 and N is 100,000, one abnormal cell is expected on average, yet the probability of observing none is about 36.8%. Actual MRD assays also depend on marker accuracy, dilution, sample processing and analytical criteria. This elementary model illustrates the distinction between a negative result and complete absence.

An association between MRD and prognosis does not establish that reducing MRD with a new treatment must yield a proportionate OS gain. A prognostic factor, a factor that predicts treatment response, and a surrogate endpoint for clinical benefit each require distinct validation.

## Event-free survival, overall survival and cure rates

EFS measures time to a defined event, whereas OS measures time to death. Check what each trial includes under EFS. Treatment failure or relapse may qualify, but definitions vary. The reported medians of 14.5 and 6.2 months cannot be read as average life expectancy or cure rates for the two treatments.

The survival function S(t) is the probability of passing time t without the defined event. The instantaneous hazard h(t) gives the event rate just after t among those still event-free. In a sufficiently smooth continuous-time model the following relationship links them.

<Math display tex={String.raw`h(t)=\lim_{\Delta t\to0^+}\frac{P(t\le T<t+\Delta t\mid T\ge t)}{\Delta t},\qquad S(t)=\exp\!\left(-\int_0^t h(s)\,ds\right)`} label="Accumulating instantaneous hazard over time relates it to the survival function" />

A constant hazard ratio over time does not imply a constant absolute difference in survival probabilities. The median marks a single point where a curve reaches 0.5. Two curves with the same median can conceal different early toxicity or late relapse patterns.

### Median plots and survival curves answer different questions

Published medians and confidence intervals can be replotted as points and intervals. Two medians alone cannot generate the actual Kaplan–Meier curves. A figure created without original follow-up or published curve data must identify its illustrative purpose, as the hypothetical curve here does.

A BCL-2 structure from another study can similarly explain the drug target without depicting PARADIGM's clinical effect. Investigators' interviews or institutional accounts can clarify the reasoning behind the design. Reading the paper's comparators and endpoints is still necessary. The question each figure answers matters more than the number of figures.

## Questions after a promising result

Longer follow-up should establish whether effects persist, how OS and treatment burden compare, and how subsequent transplant or salvage treatment contributes to the results. Randomizing the initial strategy can capture effects of the later course of care. That is different from isolating the molecular action of a single drug.

Read subgroups by the same principle. A better-looking point estimate in one group cannot establish benefit exclusively in that group. Consider sample sizes, confidence intervals, prespecification and tests for interaction across groups to determine how broadly the finding applies.
