## Once light creates a charge, how does the electron reach the electrode?

In a solar cell, the active layer absorbs light first. Here it is a thin film containing a blend of an electron donor and an electron acceptor. Of the charges generated after absorption, electrons travel to the cathode above the film and positively charged holes to the anode below. Generating charges is only the first step. When they encounter a barrier or turn back at an electrode, less electricity reaches the external circuit.

The researchers placed a thin connecting layer between the active layer and the cathode. This cathode interlayer (CIL) controls the conditions under which electrons cross the interface. It can lower the barrier to extraction and produce more uniform contact. Establishing that the barrier actually fell requires several measurements, including surface potential, conductivity and current–voltage curves.

Could changing the order of deposition improve performance while keeping the materials the same? The researchers first applied a thin coating of methanol to the active layer, then deposited molecules from the perylene diimide (PDI) family using a different solvent. The paper reports an independently certified power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 20.65% and a fill factor (FF) of 82.72% for a binary active-layer device. The certificate covers a specific laboratory cell. Large modules and factory production would require their own measurements.

**How to read this study.** Start with the efficiency figures and the deposition sequence, then ask how strongly the measurements support the interpretation of molecular orientation. The equations and angle conventions follow where they are needed.

## Two concepts to start with

Power conversion efficiency is the fraction of incident light power converted into electricity. The usual solar-cell definition multiplies open-circuit voltage, short-circuit current density and fill factor and divides by incident light power density.

<CandidateEquation lang="en" id="solar-pce-efficiency" title="How do we calculate the fraction of incident light converted into electricity?" tex={String.raw`\mathrm{PCE}=\frac{V_{OC}J_{SC}FF}{P_{in}}`}>
  <p><strong>Source and location.</strong> This standard definition predates the present Nature Energy paper. Equation (1) on page 1 of Siopa et al., <em>Scientific Reports</em> (2020), DOI <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-020-71717-0">10.1038/s41598-020-71717-0</a>, defines efficiency as the product of V<sub>oc</sub>, J<sub>sc</sub> and FF divided by incident optical power density P<sub>in</sub>. This explanation restates the definition to help interpret PCE in a performance table.</p>
  <p><strong>Symbols and units.</strong> V<sub>OC</sub> is the open-circuit voltage (volts, V). J<sub>SC</sub> is current at zero voltage divided by cell area (A cm<sup>−2</sup> or mA cm<sup>−2</sup>). FF is the dimensionless ratio of maximum power to the rectangular reference power V<sub>OC</sub> × I<sub>SC</sub>. P<sub>in</sub> is incident optical power density (W cm<sup>−2</sup>). The area normalization distinguishes J<sub>SC</sub> from current I<sub>SC</sub>.</p>
  <p><strong>Interpretation and assumptions.</strong> The numerator expresses maximum output power density P<sub>max</sub> on the measured current–voltage curve as V<sub>OC</sub>J<sub>SC</sub>FF. The denominator gives the incident optical power per unit area. Voltage times current density has units of W cm<sup>−2</sup>, matching P<sub>in</sub>. Their ratio is the efficiency. Comparisons require consistent illumination intensity, spectrum and definitions of effective area.</p>
  <p><strong>Worked example.</strong> <em>Assume</em> V<sub>OC</sub> = 0.80 V, J<sub>SC</sub> = 25 mA cm<sup>−2</sup>, FF = 0.75 and P<sub>in</sub> = 100 mW cm<sup>−2</sup>. Then PCE = 0.80 × 25 × 0.75 / 100 = 15%. These hypothetical values illustrate the units.</p>
  <p><strong>Limits.</strong> PCE measures maximum electrical output relative to incident optical power. By itself it cannot establish the cause of molecular orientation, stability, outdoor lifetime or module manufacturing yield. The conditions and values for this paper are in its tables and certification data.</p>

The interlayer changes the energy barrier and resistance where charges move from the active layer to the electrode. This differs from increasing light absorption. A rise in efficiency alone cannot establish that molecular orientation caused it. Interface structure, potential, charge transport and device measurements need to tell a consistent story.

## What changed across the three methods?

The team compared three PDI-family interlayers, PDINN, PDISNN and PDI2SNN. Method I dissolved the interlayer powder in trifluoroethanol (TFE), a polar solvent, and deposited it directly on the active layer. Method II treated the resulting interlayer with methanol. Method III reversed the order by treating the active surface with methanol first, then applying the interlayer dissolved in TFE.

The design assumes that methanol changes the active surface while TFE carries the amphiphilic interlayer molecules in solution. The authors propose that residual methanol interacts with the interlayer's polar side chains, helping the molecules stand more upright on the substrate. They infer this mechanism by combining measurements and molecular dynamics simulations. No single measurement images the entire process directly.

## From wetting to orientation

Before methanol pretreatment, droplets of the PDINN, PDISNN and PDI2SNN solutions in TFE made contact angles of 28.0°, 29.4° and 29.7° on the active layer. After pretreatment the angles fell to 12.1°, 13.6° and 14.4°. Supplementary Figure 10a shows the PDI-containing TFE droplets. Water and glycerol droplets appear separately in 10b. The greater spreading supports improved contact between the interlayer solution and the treated surface.

The authors used surface-sensitive sum-frequency generation (SFG) spectroscopy to measure vibrational signals from the PDI core. Changes in those signals carry information about average molecular orientation. Other factors can affect intensity as well, so the study also reports near-edge X-ray absorption fine structure (NEXAFS), grazing-incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering (GIWAXS), surface potential measurements and molecular dynamics calculations.

The two angle conventions here refer to different directions. In SFG, the tilt is the angle between the perylene core's C=C vibrational transition dipole and the surface normal. A smaller angle indicates a more upright core. In NEXAFS, the angle is between the normal to the molecular plane (the π* orbital vector) and the surface normal. In that convention, 90° corresponds to an edge-on molecular plane perpendicular to the surface. Compare treatment trends within each technique. Subtracting or averaging the absolute angles across techniques has no direct physical interpretation.

<NewsFigure media="oct22-dci-fig-3" lang="en" />

Figure 3 combines SFG spectra (a–d), experimental geometry (e), Kelvin probe force microscopy (KPFM) surface potentials (f) and the authors' energy-level interpretation (g). The three curves in the SFG panels represent different polarization combinations. Panel g illustrates the proposed connection between measured potential and energy alignment. The KPFM color and potential changes provide evidence about surface potential. Contact resistance and device efficiency require separate measurements.

Together these observations support a consistent interpretation. Method III showed a tendency toward more upright orientation alongside changes in surface potential and resistance and improved device performance. The materials reviewed here do not include an independent replication that excludes other routes, such as methanol-induced changes in active-layer microstructure. The measurements and model support the mechanism within the study. Independent reproduction of the whole causal chain remains open.

## A separate equation for film conductivity

The conductivity equation in the paper's Methods describes a separate measurement used to compare electrical transport in interlayer films.

<CandidateEquation lang="en" id="solar-interlayer-conductivity" title="How does the thin-film conductivity equation combine geometry and measurement?" tex={String.raw`\sigma=\frac{I L}{V W T}`}>
  <p><strong>Relation to the source.</strong> Page 8 of the Methods, under “Material characterization,” prints the unnumbered relation “σ = I × L / (V × W × T).” The symbols and order follow the original. Grouping G = I/V below is an algebraic aid for the reader.</p>
  <p><strong>Symbols and dimensions.</strong> I is measured current (A), V applied voltage (V), L electrode spacing (500 μm), W electrode width (11,000 μm) and T film thickness (about 100 nm). I/V gives conductance in siemens. L/(WT) is length divided by area, giving conductivity in S m<sup>−1</sup> or, after conversion, S cm<sup>−1</sup>. Convert L, W and T to the same unit of length before calculating.</p>
  <p><strong>What it computes.</strong> Multiplying the measured I/V by the geometric factor L/(WT) estimates the material layer's conductivity. This idealizes the film as a uniform rectangular conductor, with current passing through a cross section of width times thickness and voltage measured across length L. The paper gives electrode width and spacing without specifying an interdigitated geometry.</p>
  <p><strong>Worked example.</strong> Assuming I = 10 μA, V = 0.50 V, L = 500 μm, W = 11,000 μm and T = 100 nm gives σ ≈ 9.09 S m<sup>−1</sup> = 0.0909 S cm<sup>−1</sup>. The current and voltage in this example are hypothetical.</p>
  <p><strong>Limits.</strong> Contact resistance, nonuniform film thickness or width, and departures of the current path from the simple geometry can all affect the estimate. Film conductivity and cell PCE measure different properties. High conductivity alone cannot establish the cause of an efficiency change.</p>
</CandidateEquation>

After unit conversion, <Math tex={String.raw`\sigma`} /> gives conductivity. The Methods give electrode width and spacing without specifying an interdigitated electrode. The resulting film conductivity is distinct from the calculation of cell PCE.

## Champion cells, averages and certification

For the PM6:BTP-eC9 binary active layer, the champion Method III PDISNN cell in Table 1 reached 18.25% PCE, 0.85 V open-circuit voltage, 27.23 mA cm⁻² short-circuit current density and 78.40% FF. The paper reports 17.85 ± 0.19% across ten cells. Recalculation from the ten PCE values in the Source Data gives a mean of 17.848% and a sample standard deviation of 0.191%, matching the table after rounding. The Method I PDISNN mean was 14.36 ± 0.35%. The 18.25% figure belongs to the best device.

Its 20.72% is the best result in the paper's table. The legend in Figure 4a also identifies the conventional method as a comparison. Panel d shows the molecular structures of other PDI-based cathode interlayers. Panel e compares PCE across several CILs under stepwise deposition (Method III) and the conventional process using methanol as the processing solvent.

The generality comparison reports ten-device distributions for each active-layer combination. Recalculating from the individual D18:L8-BO Source Data gives a mean of 20.20 ± 0.34% and a maximum of 20.72%. This small laboratory sample characterizes fabricated cells. Large-area manufacturing yield requires different data. The main text reports 19.20% for a 1 cm² cell, while the supplementary graph labels it 19.19%. This small difference in reported precision represents one result. The main text compares it with a 19.32% literature benchmark for a ternary cell.

T80, the time to reach 80% of initial efficiency, was 984 hours with Method III and 330 hours with Method I. The test used encapsulated devices in air at about 25°C under continuous white LED light (410–850 nm, 100 mW cm⁻²). Hourly J–V curves were used to reset the maximum power point. These results compare operational stability under the specified laboratory illumination, temperature and encapsulation conditions. Outdoor lifetime, accelerated heat and humidity tests, and module durability lie outside that measurement.

## What does the evidence establish?

The study follows a process change through wetting, orientation signals, surface potential, transport and device performance, and supplies separate performance certification. It demonstrates laboratory cells and a 1 cm² device. Roll-to-roll processing, large-module uniformity, manufacturing yield and prolonged outdoor exposure remain untested. Solvent recovery, safety and environmental costs for methanol and TFE would also need evaluation under manufacturing conditions.

Several changes may accompany high PCE and a shift in molecular orientation. The authors acknowledge that other factors can influence SFG intensity and supplement it with NEXAFS, GIWAXS and molecular dynamics. The simulations also simplify the system, for example by fixing the substrate. The narrow conclusion is that methanol pretreatment followed by TFE-based interlayer deposition coincided with a change in orientation, modified interface properties and better performance. The authors explain these observations in terms of methanol-directed molecular assembly. Independent replication and a production-scale demonstration remain open.

## Check your understanding

**Question.** Of 20.72%, 20.65% and 20.20 ± 0.34%, which was independently certified, and what does each figure describe?

**Answer.** The independently certified result was 20.65%. The 20.72% figure belongs to one champion cell. The 20.20 ± 0.34% figure gives the mean and sample standard deviation across ten cells.
