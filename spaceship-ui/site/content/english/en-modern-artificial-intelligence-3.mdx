# Research update: August 18, 2026

> This is not PDF source material. It extends the perceptron, MLP, CNN, and convolution topics through peer-reviewed papers, proceedings, and official implementation documents available by August 18, 2026, without rewriting source claims retroactively.

## 1. Activation and normalization extend far beyond ReLU alone

The lectures introduce sign and ReLU. Practice now includes **GELU, SiLU/Swish, gated and task-specific activations**, while normalization choices include LayerNorm, RMSNorm, GroupNorm, and Global Response Normalization (GRN), selected for architecture and batch regime.

- **BatchNorm — ICML 2015, peer-reviewed:** a foundational method stabilizing training through minibatch statistics. [PMLR paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v37/ioffe15.html)
- **RMSNorm — NeurIPS 2019, peer-reviewed:** removes recentering and normalizes by root mean square, simplifying LayerNorm computation. [Official proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2019/hash/1e8a19426224ca89e83cef47f1e7f53b-Abstract.html)
- **ConvNeXt V2 — CVPR 2023, peer-reviewed:** jointly designs a fully convolutional masked autoencoder and GRN to strengthen interchannel feature competition. [CVF paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/html/Woo_ConvNeXt_V2_Co-Designing_and_Scaling_ConvNets_With_Masked_Autoencoders_CVPR_2023_paper.html)
- **Transformers without Normalization — CVPR 2025, peer-reviewed:** Dynamic Tanh replaces normalization with an elementwise tanh mapping, reporting comparable or better results in several vision/language settings. This challenges universal necessity claims without providing a universal removal method for all CNNs and MLPs. [CVF paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/html/Zhu_Transformers_without_Normalization_CVPR_2025_paper.html)
- **Dynamic Group Normalization — CVPR 2025, peer-reviewed:** adapts channel groups during training according to statistics rather than fixing them. [CVF paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/html/Smadar_Dynamic_Group_Normalization_Spatio-Temporal_Adaptation_to_Evolving_Data_Statistics_CVPR_2025_paper.html)
- **Stronger Normalization-Free Transformers — CVPR 2026, peer-reviewed:** systematically compares pointwise stabilizing functions after DyT, expanding normalization-free design beyond one tanh alternative. [CVF paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/html/Chen_Stronger_Normalization-Free_Transformers_CVPR_2026_paper.html)
- **AcTTA — CVPR 2026, peer-reviewed:** adapts ReLU/GELU thresholds and gradient sensitivity at test time rather than relying only on normalization affine parameters, making activations themselves targets of shift adaptation. [CVF paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/html/Kim_AcTTA_Rethinking_Test-Time_Adaptation_via_Dynamic_Activation_CVPR_2026_paper.html)

Evaluate activation and normalization together with **gradient scale, residual paths, batch size, pretraining objectives, and hardware kernels**, rather than as isolated decorations.

## 2. CNN versus Transformer: combining biases and recipes rather than declaring an intrinsically superior operator

- **ConvNeXt — CVPR 2022, peer-reviewed:** progressively modernizes ResNet with Transformer-era training and blocks, showing pure ConvNets can compete with Swin-family models. [CVF paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022/html/Liu_A_ConvNet_for_the_2020s_CVPR_2022_paper.html)
- **ConvNeXt V2 — CVPR 2023:** demonstrates joint architecture and self-supervised-pretraining design rather than separate optimization.
- **ShiftwiseConv — CVPR 2025, peer-reviewed:** decomposes large-kernel benefits into feature granularity and multipath fusion, building long-range sparse dependencies with small 3×3 kernels. [CVF paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/html/Li_ShiftwiseConv_Small_Convolutional_Kernel_with_Large_Kernel_Effect_CVPR_2025_paper.html)
- **MobileNetV4 — ECCV 2024, peer-reviewed:** integrates inverted bottlenecks, ConvNeXt, FFNs, and extra depthwise designs in one Universal Inverted Bottleneck search block and evaluates accelerator-aware attention. [ECVA paper](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2024/papers_ECCV/html/5647_ECCV_2024_paper.php)
- **ConvNeur — CVPR 2026, peer-reviewed:** separates global reasoning and locality-preserving representations into gated branches, treating CNNs and attention as complementary roles. [CVF paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/html/Yang_Efficiency_Follows_Global-Local_Decoupling_CVPR_2026_paper.html)

Comparisons require matched **throughput, latency, memory bandwidth, accelerator mapping, detection/segmentation transfer, pretraining data, and recipes**, beyond parameter counts or ImageNet top-1 alone.

## 3. Gradient descent selects more than just any minimum: implicit bias and feature learning

Perceptron updates specify a rule, but overparameterized networks admit many equal-training-loss solutions. Selection depends on optimizer, initialization, parameterization, batch size, and weight decay.

- **Soudry et al. — JMLR 2018, peer-reviewed:** prove max-margin implicit bias: gradient-descent directions converge to the hard-margin SVM solution in separable logistic regression. [JMLR paper](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v19/18-188.html)
- **Tensor Programs IV — ICML 2021, peer-reviewed:** gives parameterization-dependent conditions retaining feature learning rather than a kernel regime at infinite width. [PMLR paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/yang21c.html)
- **Critical feature learning — ICML 2024, peer-reviewed:** analyzes wide-network feature-learning regimes through scaling. [PMLR paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/bordelon24a.html)
- **SGD with Weight Decay Secretly Minimizes the Ranks of Your Neural Networks — CPAL 2025, peer-reviewed:** reports theoretical and experimental low-rank bias strengthened by small batches, large learning rates, and strong decay. [PMLR paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v280/galanti25a.html)
- **How Does the ReLU Activation Affect the Implicit Bias… — COLT 2026, peer-reviewed:** analyzes conditions for near-minimum-ℓ₂-norm interpolation and rapid activation-pattern stabilization in shallow ReLU regression on high-dimensional random data. Activations and data spectra jointly shape implicit bias. [PMLR paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v336/lai26a.html)
- **When Both Layers Learn — COLT 2026, peer-reviewed:** studies dynamics toward global minima from random initialization and sample complexity when both layers of a one-hidden-layer ReLU network learn, providing a simplified end-to-end feature-learning theory. [PMLR paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v336/tinaz26a.html)

Reducing training loss and learning particular representations are separate questions. Extend the lecture's updates by measuring **margins, norms, rank, feature drift, and lazy/rich regimes**.

## 4. Efficient convolution: from separability to depthwise, grouped, and reparameterized designs

PDF p. 9 reduces M² operations to 2M through separability. Modern CNNs extend efficiency choices into channels.

- **Depthwise convolution** filters each channel spatially; **pointwise 1×1 convolution** mixes channels.
- **Grouped convolution** partitions channels to control connections and computation.
- **Structural reparameterization** trains multiple branches and algebraically fuses them into one convolution before inference. RepVGG exemplifies this, followed by mobile and large-kernel architectures. [RepVGG, CVPR 2021](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2021/html/Ding_RepVGG_Making_VGG-Style_ConvNets_Great_Again_CVPR_2021_paper.html)
- **MobileNetV4** codesigns blocks for Pareto frontiers on CPUs, DSPs, GPUs, and NPUs.
- **ShiftwiseConv** offers another route to large-kernel effects through small operators.
- **PFGNet — CVPR 2026, peer-reviewed:** decomposes spatially adaptive large-kernel responses into separable `1×k` and `k×1` convolutions, reducing per-channel cost from `O(k²)` to `O(2k)`. This directly extends classical separability into dynamic receptive-field design. [CVF paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/html/Cai_PFGNet_A_Fully_Convolutional_Frequency-Guided_Peripheral_Gating_Network_for_Efficient_CVPR_2026_paper.html)

Lower FLOPs do not guarantee lower latency. Measure memory access, kernel launches, fusion, group counts, and target hardware together.

## 5. What PyTorch convolution actually computes

The lecture moves from kernel-flipped mathematical convolution to PyTorch `Conv2d`. Keep these conventions separate.

- Official PyTorch `Conv2d` computes **valid 2D cross-correlation**, without mathematical kernel flipping. [Official documentation](https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/stable/generated/torch.nn.Conv2d.html)
- `ConvTranspose2d` can be viewed as the input gradient of `Conv2d` but **is not the actual inverse of convolution**. With stride greater than one, it inserts zeros between input elements. [Official documentation](https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/stable/generated/torch.nn.ConvTranspose2d.html)
- `MaxPool2d` padding is conceptually negative infinity, preventing zero padding from incorrectly becoming the maximum for negative inputs. [Official documentation](https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/stable/generated/torch.nn.MaxPool2d.html)
- `Dropout` zeros elements with a Bernoulli mask during training and scales retained values by `1/(1-p)`, yielding identity behavior during evaluation. [Official documentation](https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/stable/generated/torch.nn.Dropout.html)

When comparing papers, DSP libraries, and frameworks, specify **flipping, padding, tensor layout, groups, dilation, bias, and train/eval mode**.

## Key terms

- **Perceptron convergence:** finite-mistake or convergence properties under linear separability and specified update rules.
- **Feature learning:** hidden representations adapting to the task rather than merely fitting linear models over fixed kernels.
