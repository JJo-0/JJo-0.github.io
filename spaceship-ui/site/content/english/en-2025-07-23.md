> **English-edition review note (2026-11-09):** Historical selections are retained with explicit qualifications. Excess B6 can cause neuropathy; NIH lists US/EFSA adult upper limits of 100mg/12mg daily, not targets. Common MTHFR variants do not require avoiding folic acid. Hydroxocobalamin requires conversion to B12 coenzyme forms. Sources: [NIH B6](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminB6-HealthProfessional/), [CDC folate](https://www.cdc.gov/folic-acid/data-research/mthfr/index.html), [NIH B12](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminB12-HealthProfessional/).

                <p class="text-red-800 text-sm leading-relaxed mb-4">
                    The original suggests <strong>GC B-Max Meta or B-Max Metabi</strong>, citing 100mg B1/B2/B6 and rapid energy-related effects.<sup>28</sup> Symptoms alone do not justify that regimen; assess their cause and B6 exposure with a professional.

                    <span class="w-6 h-6 bg-blue-200 rounded-full flex items-center justify-center text-blue-800 text-sm font-bold mr-3">2</span>

                </h4>
                <p class="text-blue-800 text-sm leading-relaxed mb-4">
