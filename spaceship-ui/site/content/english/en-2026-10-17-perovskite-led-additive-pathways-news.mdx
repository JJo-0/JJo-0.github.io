## In one sentence

By separating the pathways through which 5-aminovaleric acid (5AVA) forms a beneficial organoamidinium zwitterion and a detrimental lactam byproduct in a perovskite precursor, the researchers identified which reaction product to introduce deliberately as an additive.

## Why the distinction matters

An additive is a substance introduced in small amounts to influence crystal formation or defects in the light-emitting material. The precursor is the starting material before film formation. A byproduct arises through another reaction during processing. Assuming that the substance added at the start is also the one that acts during film formation can lead to a misleading explanation for a performance change.

Perovskite LED (PeLED) additives influence both crystal growth and defects, yet can react in solution to form other species. The team identified pathways that convert 5AVA into beneficial 5IMAP and detrimental 2PP. They synthesized 5IMAP directly and added it selectively, separating the active species from the byproduct that had previously been present in the same mixture.

## Evidence and comparison conditions

### How were the reaction products identified?

Supplementary Figure 2 compares measured mass-to-charge ratios (m/z) and isotope spacings with those expected for the proposed species. Tables 1 and 2 explicitly identify some impurity assignments as predictions based on that information and the precursor–product relationships. Assigning a signal to a candidate molecule leaves room for uncertainty in its structure. Table 2 also lists an unidentified constituent.

Unreacted 5AVA fell below the detection limit under every condition in the same table. A measurement below that limit leaves open the possibility of a small residual concentration. For Figure 4, the nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) discussion says the absence of a clear signal in the usual carboxylic-acid proton region supports a 5IMAP structure with a negatively charged carboxyl group. That observation bears on the structure. LED lifetime and the full reaction pathway need independent evidence.

**Check your understanding.** Do an undetected starting material and tentatively assigned byproducts establish every reaction? Account for the detection limit, the scope of each structural assignment and the unidentified component, then consult complementary measurements and controls.

The official abstract reports a **97.2%** mean peak photoluminescence quantum yield (PLQY) for films, a **37.1%** external quantum efficiency (EQE) for the best device and a <strong>32.8%</strong> mean peak EQE. The 37.1% value describes a single device. The researchers say they extended the chemistry to other colors and additives. The detailed controls and statistics remain to be checked in the full text.

## Does a longer emission lifetime imply lower loss?

### Which points on an efficiency curve are comparable?

The right-hand graph of Supplementary Figure 16 (PDF page 24, printed page 23) plots EQE against current density. Its horizontal scale is logarithmic, so equal distances represent different absolute changes in current density. The curves rise to a maximum and then decline. Peak EQE and performance at high current therefore characterize different operating points.

All four curves use 5IMAP but differ in their FAI formulation ratios. The identity of the additive alone does not specify the fabrication conditions. The left panel plots current density and radiance against voltage on separate vertical scales. Current density is current per unit area. Radiance gives optical output per unit area and solid angle. Match each curve to its axis before comparing efficiency, output and lifetime. This reading establishes the axes and conditions. It neither extracts new precision values from the curves nor determines the best device’s lifetime.

Supplementary Note 1 explains why emission decay time alone is insufficient for comparing films. Charges temporarily trapped at defects can recombine later, and emitted photons can be reabsorbed and re-emitted. Either effect can lengthen the decay even when losses remain appreciable.

Printed page 22 combines PLQY with mean decay time.

These two effective rates differ from the ABC model’s k₁, k₂ and k₃ in the supplement. Both effective rates have units of s⁻¹. The three ABC coefficients have units of s⁻¹, cm³·s⁻¹ and cm⁶·s⁻¹, respectively, because they multiply different powers of carrier density. Their numerical magnitudes cannot be compared directly. ## Limitations and counterevidence

PLQY measures photons emitted by an optically excited film per photon absorbed. EQE measures photons that leave an electrically driven device per electron injected. The 97.2% film result and 37.1% device result refer to different samples and denominators. The sample sizes and measurement conditions behind the best and mean device values remain to be checked against the main text and supplement.

Supplementary Table 7 (PDF page 29, printed page 28) gives an operating stability T50 of **23.3 hours at a current density of 100 mA/cm²**. T50 is the time for light output to drop to half its initial value. The abstract alone gives no lifetime, and this review has not confirmed whether the lifetime and the 37.1% peak EQE in that table were measured on the same individual device. Drive conditions, encapsulation and lifetime curves need further checking before comparison with household lighting or displays. Large-area manufacturing, thermal and humidity stability, and production yield require separate validation. Different additives may follow different pathways. The complete figures, methods and supplement remain to be reviewed for direct evidence of the mechanism and the strength of the controls.

**Check your understanding.** Do near-100% film PLQY and high peak device EQE establish a long-lived product? Optical excitation of a film and electrical operation of a device probe different samples. Initial efficiency and degradation over time also answer separate questions. Report operating conditions such as current density with any lifetime value.
