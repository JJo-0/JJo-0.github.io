**In brief.** Chest X-ray AI can quickly flag images that warrant review for possible tuberculosis (TB). Confirming TB requires additional evidence.

## First distinguish screening from confirmation

A chest radiograph shows structures inside the body. Changes associated with TB can resemble changes from other causes. An image alone cannot identify the organism responsible.

Screening examines many people or images to find **who needs further assessment**. AI can mark regions that look unusual or patterns suggestive of TB. A clinician can use that information to decide what to investigate.

Confirmation draws on additional evidence, including molecular tests. At the Mponeng mine in South Africa, Google says people flagged by the AI are referred for confirmatory molecular testing. The company describes a portable digital X-ray system and offline AI at that site. This illustrates how screening can connect to follow-up without an internet connection. Other facilities may have different equipment and working conditions. WHO likewise lists chest X-rays and computer-aided reading as screening tools and discusses rapid molecular diagnostic tests separately.

The sequence is:

**Chest X-ray → AI flags a finding for review → a clinician reviews the finding and decides what to do next → confirmatory testing when indicated**

Each stage answers a different question. An X-ray flag alone establishes neither a diagnosis nor a treatment outcome.

## How does computer-aided reading compare with a human reader?

Traditionally, a trained reader identifies abnormal findings on a chest X-ray. Computer-aided detection (CAD) analyzes the same image and can produce a score reflecting the likelihood of TB. Images above a chosen threshold are referred for further evaluation. Reading capacity can be a bottleneck where trained staff are scarce.

WHO's 2021 guidance conditionally recommended CAD to interpret digital chest X-rays for screening and triage in people aged 15 or older in populations for whom TB screening is recommended. The certainty of evidence was low. WHO called for external validation of individual products and threshold calibration in the intended setting. Its evaluation compared CAD and human readers within the same studies. Investigators set the CAD threshold to detect 90% of bacteriologically confirmed pulmonary TB cases, then compared specificity, the proportion of people without TB correctly identified as such. This operating point made comparisons possible. Performance can vary by product and setting. The recommendation concerns a class of tools and does not validate the accuracy or impact of this Nexus deployment. [WHO guidance, section 3.2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK569339/#ch3.s2)

## The figures Google reported from the field

On October 23, 2026, Google Research described its collaboration with Nexus AI. It reported operations at about 40 health facilities, where more than 90 health workers had assessed over 25,000 chest X-rays. The same paragraph described a change in turnaround from weeks to under 45 seconds.

Google named South Africa, Sierra Leone and Côte d’Ivoire, then described expansion underway in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Zambia and Cameroon. Six countries appear in the account, three of them as expansion sites. It gave no country-by-country counts of active facilities or performance estimates.

Sensitivity is the proportion of people with TB whose screening result is positive. Specificity is the proportion of people without TB whose result is negative. Both matter when weighing missed cases against unnecessary follow-up.

The 25,000 figure counts **X-ray images assessed**. The announcement did not report counts of confirmed cases or people starting treatment. Nor did it provide country-specific sensitivity and specificity, false positives, confirmatory-test completion rates, time to treatment or patient outcomes. The partners documented deployment and workflow. Clinical impact needs separate evidence.

## Which part takes 45 seconds?

Nexus describes under 45 seconds **from uploading one chest X-ray to receiving AI findings**. That interval does not cover image acquisition, specimen collection, molecular testing, clinical review, consultation and treatment initiation.

The sources support two distinct observations: the manufacturer's upload-to-finding time and Google's account of a workflow change. Public documents did not define the start and end of the earlier “weeks” interval or establish whether it was measured in the same facilities, samples and conditions. Dividing the two figures would not give a meaningful speedup. Neither confirms a diagnosis in 45 seconds or shows that treatment began weeks sooner.

## Regulatory scope varies by feature

Nexus describes Nexus AI CXR as a CE MDR Class IIb medical device that highlights findings outside normal lung appearances and patterns suggestive of TB to support clinician judgment. Its description says clinical interpretation and diagnosis still rest with the clinician. Google said WHO prequalification was being pursued and work on the US FDA process was underway. These are separate regulatory milestones.

Nexus labels multilabel abnormality classification and LLM-assisted reporting as Beta features outside the stated CE MDR Class IIb certification for the core product.

WHO's 2025 CAD policy document records that Nexus AI v1.0.0, submitted by Nexus Intelligence, was excluded from performance analysis because it did not satisfy the regulatory eligibility requirement for that evaluation. The manufacturer's device labeling also links Nexus AI CXR 1.0 with software version 1.0.0. This records an eligibility decision for the submitted version. WHO did not issue a performance result for it. The entry does not establish the regulatory status or performance of the version deployed in 2026. [WHO 2025 policy document](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240110373/)

## What remains uncertain?

Google's announcement describes deployment scale and field workflow. It supplies no country- or facility-level performance tables or patient-outcome study. Independent field data on false positives, referral completion and treatment outcomes would help establish what changed for patients. Google and Nexus participated in the collaboration they describe.

This article explains how to read the research and product announcements. Individual testing and care decisions require clinical assessment.

## Check your understanding

What can you infer when the AI marks an X-ray as suspicious for TB? The image contains a finding that warrants review. A clinician still needs to evaluate it and arrange confirmatory testing when indicated.

Does a reported 45-second analysis show patients received treatment sooner? It describes the interval from upload to AI findings. To assess the whole care pathway, measure time spent waiting for review, completing further tests, communicating results and starting treatment.
