> **Evidence status.** A company announcement about clinical development. It reports neither phase 3 results nor regulatory approval.

**What happened.** Rentosertib, a candidate originating in AI-assisted target and molecule discovery, **reached the milestone of dosing a patient in a phase 3 trial**. This development does not establish that AI has produced a clinically effective treatment.

Rentosertib (ISM001-055, INS018_055) is an oral TNIK inhibitor under development for idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF). It is designed to inhibit the activity of the protein TNIK. Its mechanism does not establish patient benefit. In September 2026, Insilico Medicine announced first dosing in GENESIS-IPF-3, NCT07687459. The company described a prospective, multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial. The public registry specified quadruple masking of participants, care providers, investigators, and outcome assessors. Keep the descriptions associated with their sources. The plan was to follow lung function for 52 weeks in a target population of 320. That figure was a recruitment target.

Each design term describes a safeguard. A prospective study follows outcomes forward in time. Multiple centers participate, allocation is randomized, and the specified people are kept unaware of the assigned group. A placebo control receives an intervention without the investigational drug’s active ingredient. These features help limit bias but cannot predict a favorable result at trial initiation.

Look for the spaces and the walls between them. Microscopy and spirometry provide different kinds of information. FVC, the clinical measure discussed below, records the volume a person can forcibly exhale. It is not a count or area of spaces in the image.

A trial starting and a treatment working are different developments. The phase identifies the stage of testing. Outcomes describe what was observed. Adding sites and planning to recruit more people cannot tell us the magnitude of an effect.

A candidate discovered with AI reaching phase 3 shows that human testing has progressed well beyond a computer prediction. Human testing had already begun in earlier trials. Phase 3 is **a larger test of the candidate**. Its initiation establishes neither clinical success nor the superiority of AI discovery.

## Evidence available so far

The earlier phase 2a trial studied safety and early efficacy signals in people with IPF. Forced vital capacity (FVC) is the volume a person can forcibly exhale after a maximal inhalation, measured here in milliliters (mL). At week 12, mean FVC change in the 60 mg once-daily group was +98.4 mL (95% CI 10.9–185.9 mL). The interval describes uncertainty. This was a secondary endpoint in a small, short study.

The phase 2a primary endpoint was the proportion of participants experiencing at least one treatment-emergent adverse event. Change in lung function was secondary. The +98.4 mL is a change from baseline within the 60 mg once-daily group, not its improvement over placebo.

### Safety alongside the efficacy signal

In Table 2, investigators classified adverse events as treatment-related in 14 of 18 participants (77.8%) in the 60 mg once-daily group and 5 of 17 (29.4%) on placebo. Treatment-related serious adverse events occurred in two and zero participants, respectively. Four and two discontinued treatment because of adverse events. The small study did not perform between-group statistical tests for adverse events, so these frequencies do not establish a definitive risk difference. Evidence on safety must be read alongside the efficacy signal.

The phase 2a Methods say that about 15 participants per group would give roughly a 90% chance of observing at least one event if its true rate were around 15%. That was a sample-size assumption, not an observed event rate or a probability that the drug is safe. **For illustration**, if the true independent event probability were 1%, the chance of seeing at least one in 15 people would be only about 14%. This example does not estimate the drug’s actual rare-event rate. It explains why observing none in a small trial cannot rule out rare harm. Exclusions for health conditions and concomitant drugs also limit generalization.

### How did the analysis handle missing measurements?

Of 71 randomized participants, 16 stopped treatment and 22 lacked week-12 spirometry. Treatment discontinuation and a missing measurement at one time point are different events. Investigators used multiple imputation for missing lung function values and analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) accounting for treatment group and baseline lung function. This approach relies on assumptions about missingness given observed information. It does not recover a known measurement or remove uncertainty.

Missingness differed across groups. The 60 mg once-daily group lacked week-12 spirometry for 8 of 18 participants, compared with 3 of 17 on placebo (Methods, Statistical analysis). Interpret the assumptions and sensitivity of the missing-data analysis alongside the observed means. The counts alone do not identify why measurements were missing.

### Would the result generalize to other patients?

The trial randomized rentosertib doses and placebo. It did not separately randomize use of background antifibrotic treatment. The paper reports an FVC signal in some participants without concurrent background therapy. Concluding that the candidate was superior to existing drugs, or ineffective with them, would require a prespecified interaction analysis and an adequate sample.

All 71 randomized participants were Asian, and 64 were men (Table 1). This describes the limits of generalizing from a small sample. It does not establish that women or other populations should be excluded.

Supplementary Table S5 lists acute exacerbations in 3 of 18 participants (16.7%) in the 60 mg once-daily group and 1 of 17 (5.9%) on placebo. These are people, not total episodes: the individual histories include recurrent exacerbations. Acute worsening is clinically important, but 12 weeks in these small groups cannot establish that the drug caused exacerbations or a particular risk difference. The authors identify this as a question for larger, longer studies. Quality-of-life results were generally variable and inconclusive. Signals in selected cough measures cannot establish improvement in life overall. [Phase 2a Discussion and Supplementary Table S5.]

Phase 2a therefore did not establish that the drug restores lung function. Phase 3 asks whether the signal replicates in a larger population over longer follow-up.

Consider a **hypothetical example** of missingness. If people with missing results started with worse lung function, averaging only the remaining patients without accounting for baseline could distort the comparison. Multiple imputation creates several plausible values based on observed information and carries uncertainty into the analysis. If unrecorded worsening itself made visits less likely, the same assumptions may be insufficient. This illustrates a limitation of reconstructing the 22 missing values. It does not identify the actual reason for missingness.

Ask this when reading the result: **Does +98.4 mL mean the group improved by that amount over placebo?** A within-group change and a treatment–placebo difference use different comparisons. Interpreting an effect requires the placebo result, the analysis population, the handling of missing values, and uncertainty.

## What to look for in phase 3

First, follow recruitment and completion. The company planned to enroll 320 people across 47 sites in China. The registry, checked November 3, 2026 (KST), also listed a target of 320 and recruiting status. The actual study start was September 9. The company announcement followed on September 10. Estimated completion was October 30, 2029. Target enrollment and dates do not establish completed enrollment or follow-up.

Second, examine the rate of lung-function decline. The announced primary endpoint was the annual rate of FVC decline over 52 weeks. The registry specifies volume in mL. The comparison concerns how much the volume a patient can forcibly exhale changes over a year. It is distinct from a percentage change. FVC captures one aspect of lung function. The company also lists time to first disease progression event as a key secondary endpoint. Both were planned outcomes, not phase 3 observations.

Third, assess safety when the drug is used with existing antifibrotic treatment. Patients may already be taking nintedanib or pirfenidone, making concomitant use relevant.

Fourth, monitor liver function, interactions, and long-term safety. Using AI to identify a candidate does not answer how it behaves in the body or interacts with other drugs.

## What does “AI-discovered drug” mean here?

The company describes an AI-assisted process for target discovery and molecule design. That role in discovery is distinct from evidence of efficacy and safety in people. This review checked the prior phase 2a evidence and the initiation and design of phase 3. It did not compare every step of development with a conventionally discovered drug.

The appropriate description is **a candidate originating in an AI-assisted discovery process that has reached late-stage clinical development**. The evidence does not attribute the invention to AI alone.

## The main limitation

At the time covered here, no phase 3 results were available. Dosing the first patient is an industry milestone, not an efficacy or safety outcome.

Labels such as “world first” can depend on a company’s definition of a generative-AI-driven drug and on which uses of AI that definition includes.

## Evidence to watch

Follow completed enrollment, interim safety signals, the phase 3 primary endpoint, and any submission to regulators.

Two distinct questions matter:

> **Does this drug benefit patients compared with placebo, and are its risks acceptable?**

That is the question the trial directly addresses. Whether AI makes better drugs faster and at lower cost than conventional discovery is a question about development methods. One candidate’s success or failure cannot answer it. A comparison would need time, cost, and success rates across comparable programs, including failures. Even a positive phase 3 result would not establish both clinical value and a comparative advantage for AI development.
