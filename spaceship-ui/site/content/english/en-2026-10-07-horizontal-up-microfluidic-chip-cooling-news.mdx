## The finding in one sentence

The team embedded microfluidic channels in the chip substrate. With **horizontal liquid inlets and vertical vapor outlets**, the electrically insulating dielectric coolant HFE-7100 reached a **critical heat flux of 1,570 W/cm²**.

## How the design differs

Embedded water cooling can handle very high heat flux but requires stringent control of leakage and short circuits. Dielectric coolants provide electrical insulation, yet have lower specific heat and thermal conductivity and can be difficult to clear of vapor. Comparisons must account for area, flow and pressure as well as coolant. The supplement lists earlier HFE-7100 manifold microchannel results of 1,000 and 1,020 W/cm². Earlier dielectric methods did not all fall below 1,000 W/cm².

The horizontal-up manifold rapidly replenishes liquid from the side and sends vapor produced by boiling directly upward. The design **separates the flow directions for liquid replenishment and vapor removal**.

## Reading the units in 1,570 W/cm²

Heat flux is a heat transfer rate divided by area. A watt transfers one joule per second. The cm² in the denominator identifies the area used. The chip need not consume 1,570 W in total. To convert heat flux to a total heat removal rate, we need the heated area.

The abstract reports a critical heat flux (CHF). To interpret this limiting point or compare devices, consider the heated area, flow, pressure, inlet temperature, wall temperature and uncertainty. It does not prescribe a continuous operating point or measure data-center efficiency. The Methods report a 3 × 3 mm² active area (0.09 cm²) and an inlet at 23 ± 1°C. The authors calibrated heat losses at thermal equilibrium without coolant flow using heater power and the mean temperature of nine RTDs. Losses were below 1% of total power in the HUMMC tests. Figure 2 marks the abrupt change in chip temperature and thermal resistance after CHF. The sections reviewed give neither a numerical detection criterion nor the heater step size. Pressure drop was measured across the device under test (DUT). The exact pressure-tap positions and absolute outlet pressure were unspecified.

## Finding the conditions of the peak in the public data

Supplementary Table 4 classifies the device as a silicon manifold microchannel (MMC) using HFE-7100. Its row reports a 9 mm² test area, qmax of 1,573 W/cm², ΔTsat of 44°C, Tw of 111°C and pressure drop of 81 kPa. Tw is the mean temperature of the microchannel floor estimated using conduction resistance. At maximum CHF the main text reports RTD-based ΔTmax ≈ 130 K and ΔTmean ≈ 110 K. Tw is a different quantity from the maximum RTD temperature. The measurements and estimates have separate definitions. Scaling them to a large chip requires further tests.

The public Figure 3 data list approximately 1,572.82 W/cm² at 120 mL/min with a heat-flux uncertainty of about 28.82 W/cm². Corresponding panel data give an outlet temperature of 54.1 ± 0.5°C and pressure drop of 80.89 ± 0.29 kPa. These are matched conditions across panels, specific to that flow. The Methods define steady state as a plateau in Tout with fluctuations below 0.1 K. That criterion concerns steady state rather than detection of CHF.

Volume flow is the volume of coolant passing per unit time. Pressure drop is the reduction in pressure as fluid passes through the device. A pump must overcome that resistance to drive sufficient liquid through narrow passages. High heat flux alone does not imply low pumping demand.

### What denominators do heat flux and efficiency use?

In Methods, “Data reduction,” heat flux is q = (Q − Q_loss)/A_chip. The no-flow equilibrium calibration fits loss to the mean of nine RTDs as Q_loss = 0.0102T_mean − 0.242. Supplementary Figure 10a gives T_mean in °C and Q_loss in W, making the slope 0.0102 W/°C and the intercept −0.242 W. Methods define A_chip as the 3 × 3 mm² active heater. Supplementary Table 4 lists the same numerical area of 9 mm² without expressly identifying its A column as the denominator in this equation.

The coefficient of performance (COP) in the same table divides heat removed by volume flow times pressure drop. The denominator is hydraulic power when the units are consistent. Actual pump electricity can differ because of pump efficiency and loads in external plumbing and cooling equipment. This COP alone gives no data-center-wide energy efficiency.

Methods define ΔTmax = Tmax − Tin and ΔTmean = Tmean − Tin, then Rmax = ΔTmax/q and Rmean = ΔTmean/q. When q is in W/cm² these resistances are area normalized, in K·cm²/W. A whole-device resistance in K/W requires a different comparison. These definitions correspond to Figures 2e and 3a–b.

Methods also estimate wall temperature and superheat. The model uses one-dimensional conduction through the solid layers with R_cond = t_Si/λ_Si + t_SiO₂/λ_SiO₂, where t is layer thickness and λ is thermal conductivity. Mean floor temperature is T_w = T_mean − qR_cond, and wall superheat is ΔT_sat = T_w − T_sat,out. T_sat,out is the saturation temperature of HFE-7100 at outlet pressure P_out. With t in m and λ in W/(m·K), R_cond is in m²·K/W and q must be in W/m². Multiply a value in W/cm² by 10,000 before using it. The product qR_cond is the temperature drop in K across the solid, which is subtracted from T_mean. This model estimates temperature through the thickness. It does not image the entire wall. Methods give neither an absolute P_out nor pressure-tap coordinates.

## Removing heat and cooling uniformly

A corner can remain hot even at a moderate average temperature. Both the total heat removed and its spatial distribution matter. Supplementary Figure 2 shows nine platinum resistance temperature detectors (RTDs) at representative points on the heater. Each detects temperature through the changing electrical resistance of metal. Nine locations provide discrete readings.

The horizontal axis of Supplementary Figure 5c is heat flux and the vertical axis is the temperature standard deviation, σ_T. This measures the spread around the mean. A difference of 3 K has the same magnitude as a difference of 3°C. It is distinct from an absolute temperature of 3 K.

The authors report a spread below 3 K up to 1,100 W/cm² at 120 mL/min. The same red triangle curve rises at higher heat flux. The reported uniformity below 3 K therefore cannot be attached to the peak CHF near 1,570 W/cm². Heat removal and uniformity depend on the operating point even at the same flow.

**Check your understanding.** Does reaching the highest heat flux imply the most uniform temperature at that point? Figure 5c shows increasing spread over part of the high-flux range. Choosing continuous operating conditions requires the local distribution and allowable temperatures as well as the maximum.

## Assigning separate roles to the passages

The manifold feeds liquid horizontally and exhausts vapor upward. A manifold divides flow among smaller passages or collects it from them. Its geometry is designed to keep liquid supply and vapor evacuation from obstructing one another. The paper's figures and test conditions establish the actual flow distribution and size of the improvement.

A dielectric coolant has different electrical properties from water. Leakage, material compatibility and long-term reliability still matter. The abstract discusses potential MEMS-based integration and scaling. Production qualification and rack-wide energy savings would require separate demonstrations.

### Check your understanding

Do two coolers with the same W/cm² have equal efficiency? Check their test areas, conditions and the work the pumps require. Maximum heat flux and system efficiency answer different questions.

## Strongest evidence

- working fluid: dielectric **HFE-7100**
- critical heat flux: **1,570W/cm²**
- The maximum test point in the public source data corresponds to 120 mL/min.
- Fabrication uses a microelectromechanical systems (MEMS) process.
- Source data are public.

### The test chip and the scope of the stability test

Compare like flows first. At 60 mL/min the conventional straight parallel microchannel (SPMC) reached about 640 W/cm² and the new HUMMC about 1,200 W/cm². The peak of 1,570 W/cm² came from a separate test at 120 mL/min. The first comparison isolates architecture at one flow. The second explores maximum performance. Arrows in Figure 2 mark the sharp rise in chip temperature and thermal resistance after the critical point. The sections reviewed do not give a numerical temperature-jump rule. [Main text, Thermo-hydraulic characterization, Figures 2c–f]

The 3 × 3 mm² heated region in the main text and Methods is 0.09 cm². A platinum thin-film resistor supplied uniform heat in place of an actual processor, with nine distributed temperature sensors in the same area. Inlet coolant was held at 23 ± 1°C. Net heat flux subtracts ambient heat loss before division by area. Corrected loss remained below 1% of input across the HUMMC tests. Multiplying the rounded peak of 1,570 W/cm² by 0.09 cm² gives about 141 W net heat transfer. This area conversion uses the paper's definition. GPU power consumption was not measured.

Raising flow did not improve every metric together. Peak CHF occurred at 120 mL/min, but minimum Rmax = 0.071 and Rmean = 0.065 K·cm²/W occurred at 150 mL/min. At 150 mL/min, early CHF coincided with Tout leveling near 50°C. The authors discuss flow maldistribution and possible local dryout. A separate stability test maintained heat loads of 1,000–1,400 W/cm² for ten minutes while tracking temperature and pressure. Maximum temperature fluctuations stayed within ±2 K at 90 and 120 mL/min. Longer lifetime, leakage and thermal-cycle testing remain open. [Main text, Highest cooling performance; Methods, Thermo-hydraulic experimental setup and Data reduction]

## Main limitation

CHF is the limiting heat flux the device can tolerate. A data-center processor's mean power density, pumping power, pressure drop, coolant lifecycle and manifold reliability each require their own measurement.

The study did not assess HFE fluids' environmental impact, supply stability or regulatory suitability. Commercial suitability cannot be inferred from this result alone. Rack-level cooling energy also lies outside this chip-level demonstration.

## Why keep track of this result?

AI accelerators and power devices are constrained by **how close to the heat source the cooling can operate** as well as by their transistors. If embedded dielectric two-phase cooling meets manufacturing and long-term reliability requirements, it could encourage package and cooling design to proceed together.

## What would prompt a reassessment?

- COP including pumping power
- large-area chip·hotspot validation
- long-duration fouling·dry-out reliability
- Process integration with the package and interconnects
- rack-level energy benchmark
