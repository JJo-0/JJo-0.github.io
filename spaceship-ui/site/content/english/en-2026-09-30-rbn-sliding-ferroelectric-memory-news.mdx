**In brief.** Researchers had explored memory that changes electrical state by sliding atomic layers in small flakes. Reproducing the same stack across a wafer was harder. A September 29 paper in *Nature Nanotechnology* connects **fabrication of 4-inch rhombohedral boron nitride (rBN) wafers with ferroelectric field-effect transistor (FeFET) memory**. It is a manufacturing advance at that scale. Commercial 300 mm memory production is a separate milestone.<Cite n={1} />

## L0 — What is rBN, and why does stacking matter?

**BN** combines boron (B) and nitrogen (N). Picture a two-dimensional crystal as a very thin sheet. In <strong>rhombohedral BN (rBN)</strong>, successive sheets are shifted slightly and follow **an A→B→C sequence**, known as ABC stacking. The label rBN describes this arrangement. Hexagonal BN (hBN) contains the same elements but has a different crystal structure.<Cite n={1} />

In a conventional ferroelectric, displacements of atoms within a material can reverse its electrical polarization. In **sliding ferroelectricity**, a small relative shift between adjacent atomic layers changes their registry and polarization. Polarization is an electrical orientation arising from a displacement between the centers of positive and negative charge. If two stable orientations persist and can be read, they can encode zero and one. **The relative motion occurs on an atomic scale.**<Cite n={1} />

## L1 — How can a transistor serve as memory?

A transistor is an electrical switch. Voltage applied at the <strong>gate</strong> controls current from the <strong>source</strong> to the <strong>drain</strong>. The path between them is the <strong>channel</strong>. In a **FeFET**, polarization stored in a ferroelectric on the gate side affects how readily the channel turns on. The authors report distinct read currents in an array using rBN as the ferroelectric layer and MoS₂ as the channel.<Cite n={1} />

Here, “memory” means that the two states can still be distinguished for a time after power is removed. The **memory window** is the separation between their turn-on voltages. The **on/off ratio** compares the currents in the two states. The abstract reports a memory window of about **4 V** even at a channel length of 30 nm, an on/off ratio on the order of **10⁶**, and thermal stability above **470 K**. Each figure must be read under its measurement conditions. They do not necessarily describe a single device measured under one set of conditions.<Cite n={1} />

These two thresholds are hypothetical. The paper’s 4 V result belongs to its actual device measurement conditions.<Cite n={1} />

## L2 — Why does the 4-inch diameter matter?

Sliding layers in a small BN flake and reproducing the same arrangement across a circular wafer are different tasks. The ABC structure can give way to other, more stable BN structures, making large-area uniform growth difficult. The team formed a **nickel–boron (Ni–B) film** on **stepped sapphire** and used the surface steps to template the growth orientation in a process called **step-templated interfacial epitaxy**. The abstract reports repeated production of 4-inch rBN wafers, with structural measurements supporting phase purity and consistency across batches.<Cite n={1} />

The controls matter. According to [Extended Data Figure 3](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41565-026-02280-4/figures/7), sapphire surfaces with a different orientation favored hBN. A hexagonal phase also appeared when the growth temperature was raised to 1,050 °C. This links **the surface-step conditions to ABC stacking**, beyond simply producing a large sheet of BN. The original figure is available at the publisher.<Cite n={1} />

The abstract reports nanosecond-range FeFET switching, endurance exceeding **2×10⁹ cycles**, and ten-year nonvolatile retention. **Ten years is a long-term retention assessment or projection**, distinct from continuously observing the reported devices over a decade before the 2026 publication. The Extended Data pulse description also notes **attenuation and waveform distortion below 3 ns due to instrument bandwidth**. The measurements therefore do not establish an error-free measurement of an exact 1 ns intrinsic switching time.<Cite n={1} />

## L3 — What the study establishes and what remains open

| Level | Evidence in the paper | Question still open |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Material | 4-inch rBN wafers and structural characterization | Yield and process compatibility at 200–300 mm |
| Device | rBN FeFETs and arrays with MoS₂ channels | Cell-to-cell variation and errors in large arrays |
| Speed and retention | Nanosecond operation, cycling, and reported long-term retention | Matched comparisons accounting for instrument limits and long-duration direct measurements |
| AI | A prospective memory platform | Energy and latency on actual AI workloads |

**A 4-inch wafer is a different milestone from 300 mm CMOS volume production. A 30 nm channel is one feature of a device. Actual AI inference needs a workload benchmark.** The reported numbers establish the scope reached by the research. Its editorial candidate score of 98/100 reflects selection priority, separate from any performance metric or academic award.<Cite n={1} />
