## Key points
- Diabetes management brings together glucose monitoring, nutrition, physical activity, sleep, stress, and preparation for emergencies.
- This page offers daily routines and example review criteria people can adapt with their care team.
- Use the interactive dashboard to jump to the relevant section and tailor the plan.

## Contents
1. Dashboard: three-month goals and weekly checkpoints
2. Nutrition: carbohydrate management and meal patterns
3. Activity: setting a safe level and building a routine
4. Habits: sleep, stress, and taking prescribed medicines
5. Emergencies: checklists for low and high blood glucose

## Medical information notice
> This content is for general information. It cannot replace medical diagnosis or treatment. Discuss individual treatment decisions with your care team.

            <h3 class="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900">2.1. Macronutrients: How Food Fuels the Body</h3>
        </div>
        <div class="prose prose-lg max-w-none text-gray-700 leading-relaxed space-y-6">
            <p>
                The body uses carbohydrate, protein and fat for energy. Their balance affects blood glucose. The ADA emphasizes that there is no single ideal ratio for everyone. The proportions depend on health, needs and preferences.⁴ The following flexible ranges draw on KDA recommendations and other international guidelines.⁴
            </p>
            <div class="grid md:grid-cols-3 gap-6">
                <div class="bg-gradient-to-br from-yellow-50 to-orange-50 rounded-xl p-6 border border-yellow-200 shadow-md">

                </div>

                    <div class="text-2xl font-bold text-red-600 mb-2">15-20%</div>
                    <p class="text-red-700 text-sm">Share of total calories</p>
                </div>
                <div class="bg-gradient-to-br from-purple-50 to-indigo-50 rounded-xl p-6 border border-purple-200 shadow-md">
                    <h4 class="text-lg font-bold text-purple-800 mb-3">Fat</h4>
                    <div class="text-2xl font-bold text-purple-600 mb-2">25-35%</div>
                    <p class="text-purple-700 text-sm">Share of total calories</p>

                <h4 class="text-lg font-bold text-blue-800 mb-3">🔑 The quality of each macronutrient matters more than the percentages alone</h4>
                <ul class="space-y-3 text-blue-700">
                    <li><strong>Carbohydrate:</strong> Choose fiber-rich foods such as whole grains, vegetables and legumes more often than sugar or refined white flour. The fiber in these foods slows digestion and glucose absorption, so blood glucose rises more gradually.⁴</li>
                    <li><strong>Protein:</strong> Choose fish, skinless chicken breast, tofu or beans more often than fatty cuts of red meat.</li>
                    <li><strong>Fat:</strong> Olive oil, perilla oil, nuts and oily fish provide unsaturated fats. Substituting these for saturated and trans fats supports cardiovascular health.⁷</li>

            <h3 class="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900">2.3. Glycemic Index: Choosing Carbohydrates That Raise Glucose More Gradually</h3>
        </div>
        <div class="prose prose-lg max-w-none text-gray-700 leading-relaxed space-y-6">
            <p>
                Foods containing the same amount of carbohydrate can produce different rates of increase in blood glucose. The glycemic index (GI) describes this difference numerically.¹⁴
            </p>
            <div class="bg-gradient-to-r from-amber-100 to-yellow-100 rounded-xl p-6 border border-amber-200">
                <h4 class="text-lg font-bold text-amber-800 mb-3">🔥 An analogy</h4>
                <p class="text-amber-700 italic">“Think of the glycemic index as a speedometer for how quickly food releases energy. White rice, a higher-GI food, is like a match that flares up quickly. Brown rice, used here as a lower-GI example, is more like a log that burns slowly and steadily.”</p>
            </div>
            <p>Choosing lower-GI foods where practical can help moderate the rise in glucose after a meal.</p>
            <div class="grid md:grid-cols-2 gap-6">
                <div class="bg-green-50 rounded-xl p-6 border border-green-200">
                    <h4 class="text-lg font-bold text-green-800 mb-4">✅ Examples of lower-GI foods (55 or below)</h4>
                    <ul class="space-y-2 text-green-700">
                        <li>• Brown rice, mixed grains and whole-wheat bread</li>
                        <li>• Oats and buckwheat noodles</li>
                        <li>• Most vegetables, beans and tofu</li>
                        <li>• Apples, pears and cherries</li>
                        <li>• Unsweetened dairy products</li>
                    </ul>
                </div>
                <div class="bg-red-50 rounded-xl p-6 border border-red-200">
                    <h4 class="text-lg font-bold text-red-800 mb-4">⚠️ Examples of higher-GI foods (70 or above)</h4>
                    <ul class="space-y-2 text-red-700">
                        <li>• White rice, white bread and rice cakes</li>
                        <li>• Potatoes and corn</li>
                        <li>• Watermelon</li>
                        <li>• Sugary drinks and sweets</li>
                    </ul>
                </div>
            </div>
            <div class="bg-indigo-50 rounded-xl p-6 border border-indigo-200">
                <h4 class="text-lg font-bold text-indigo-800 mb-3">💡 How preparation affects GI</h4>
                <p class="text-indigo-700">Preparation can change a food’s GI. The guide contrasts raw carrots and steamed sweet potatoes with cooked carrots and roasted sweet potatoes, which can have a higher GI.¹⁶ Less processing, keeping foods intact and including fiber in a meal can help slow the rise in blood glucose.</p>
            </div>

            <h3 class="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900">2.4. Dietary Patterns and Strategies for Eating Out</h3>
        </div>
        <div class="prose prose-lg max-w-none text-gray-700 leading-relaxed space-y-6">
            <p>Learning a sustainable pattern of eating can be more helpful in the long run than concentrating on a single nutrient.</p>
            <div class="bg-gradient-to-r from-teal-100 to-cyan-100 rounded-xl p-6 border border-teal-200">
                <h4 class="text-lg font-bold text-teal-800 mb-4">🌊 Adapting Mediterranean and DASH eating patterns to Korean meals</h4>
                <p class="text-teal-700 mb-4">Mediterranean and DASH eating patterns, the latter developed to help manage high blood pressure, have shown benefits for cardiovascular health and insulin resistance.⁷ Both emphasize whole grains, vegetables, fruit, unsaturated fats and fish while limiting red meat and highly processed foods.</p>
                <div class="grid md:grid-cols-3 gap-4">
                    <div class="bg-white rounded-lg p-4 shadow-sm">
                        <h5 class="font-bold text-teal-800 mb-2">🫒 Choose cooking oils</h5>
                        <p class="text-sm text-teal-700">Use olive, perilla or sesame oil in stir-fries and seasoned vegetable dishes.</p>
                    </div>
                    <div class="bg-white rounded-lg p-4 shadow-sm">
                        <h5 class="font-bold text-teal-800 mb-2">🐟 Include fish more often</h5>
                        <p class="text-sm text-teal-700">Try grilled oily fish at least twice a week. Mackerel, Pacific saury and Spanish mackerel are familiar options in Korea.</p>
                    </div>
                    <div class="bg-white rounded-lg p-4 shadow-sm">
                        <h5 class="font-bold text-teal-800 mb-2">🥬 Serve plenty of vegetables</h5>
                        <p class="text-sm text-teal-700">Add seasoned vegetables (namul) in different colors and leafy greens for wraps at meals to increase fiber and vitamin intake.</p>
                    </div>
                </div>
            </div>
            <div class="border-l-4 border-orange-500 pl-6">
                <h4 class="text-xl font-bold text-orange-800 mb-4">🍻 Work dinners and restaurant meals</h4>
                <p class="text-gray-700 mb-4">Frequent work dinners are part of life for the person in this example. They are also a chance to put these principles into practice.²¹</p>
                <div class="space-y-4">
                    <div class="bg-green-50 rounded-lg p-4 border border-green-200">
                        <h5 class="font-bold text-green-800 mb-2">🥗 Start with vegetables</h5>
                        <p class="text-green-700 text-sm">While the meat cooks, start with the salad, namul, leafy greens or mushrooms on the table. Aim to fill up about halfway on these dishes. Their fiber helps you feel satisfied and can slow the rise in blood glucose.</p>
                    </div>
                    <div class="bg-blue-50 rounded-lg p-4 border border-blue-200">
                        <h5 class="font-bold text-blue-800 mb-2">🍚 Make rice and noodles a side dish</h5>
                        <p class="text-blue-700 text-sm">Large servings of rice and noodles can contribute substantially to post-meal glucose rises in a Korean diet.²³ Ask for half a bowl of rice when ordering. If a stew or hot pot comes with fried rice or extra noodles at the end, you can skip them.</p>
                    </div>
                    <div class="bg-purple-50 rounded-lg p-4 border border-purple-200">
                        <h5 class="font-bold text-purple-800 mb-2">🥩 Choose how your protein is prepared</h5>
                        <p class="text-purple-700 text-sm">Salt-grilled or plain grilled meat, boiled pork, sliced raw fish or steamed dishes are alternatives to sweetly marinated short ribs or spicy chicken (dakgalbi). Grilling and steaming are generally preferable to deep-frying.</p>
                    </div>
                    <div class="bg-amber-50 rounded-lg p-4 border border-amber-200">
                        <h5 class="font-bold text-amber-800 mb-2">🍺 Alternate alcohol and water</h5>
                        <p class="text-amber-700 text-sm">If you drink at a work dinner, have a glass of water with each alcoholic drink. This can help with hydration and slow your pace. Unsweetened distilled spirits such as plain soju or whisky generally contain less carbohydrate than sweetened fruit soju or makgeolli. Drinking heavily can cause low or high blood glucose regardless of the type of drink.⁶</p>

        <h2 class="text-3xl font-bold text-gray-900">3. Physical Activity: When and How Much to Move</h2>
    </div>
    <div class="bg-white rounded-2xl shadow-lg border border-gray-100 p-8 mb-8">
        <div class="prose prose-lg max-w-none text-gray-700 leading-relaxed space-y-6">
            <p>
                Exercise helps regulate blood glucose and can improve insulin resistance, a major factor in type 2 diabetes. These benefits go beyond weight loss and are particularly relevant for someone who spends much of the day sitting.
            </p>
        </div>
    </div>
    <!-- 3.1. The science of exercise -->
    <div class="bg-white rounded-2xl shadow-lg border border-gray-100 p-8 mb-8">
        <div class="flex items-center mb-6">
            <div class="w-10 h-10 bg-gradient-to-br from-blue-500 to-indigo-600 rounded-lg flex items-center justify-center text-white font-bold text-lg mr-3">
                🔬
            </div>
            <h3 class="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900">3.1. How Movement Helps Regulate Blood Glucose</h3>
        </div>
        <div class="prose prose-lg max-w-none text-gray-700 leading-relaxed space-y-6">
            <p>Two mechanisms explain how physical activity can lower glucose.²⁴</p>
            <div class="grid md:grid-cols-2 gap-6">
                <div class="bg-gradient-to-br from-blue-50 to-indigo-50 rounded-xl p-6 border border-blue-200">
                    <h4 class="text-lg font-bold text-blue-800 mb-3">⚙️ Greater insulin sensitivity</h4>
                    <p class="text-blue-700 text-sm">Exercise makes muscle cells more responsive to insulin. Like oil on a stiff hinge, this lets the same amount of insulin move glucose from the bloodstream into cells more effectively.</p>
                </div>
                <div class="bg-gradient-to-br from-green-50 to-emerald-50 rounded-xl p-6 border border-green-200">
                    <h4 class="text-lg font-bold text-green-800 mb-3">🔋 Glucose uptake during muscle contraction</h4>
                    <p class="text-green-700 text-sm">Contracting muscles can also take up glucose for energy through a pathway that works independently of insulin. This is especially helpful when the body does not respond fully to insulin.</p>
                </div>
            </div>
            <div class="bg-yellow-50 rounded-xl p-6 border border-yellow-200">
                <h4 class="text-lg font-bold text-yellow-800 mb-3">⏰ How long the effect can last</h4>
                <p class="text-yellow-700">Together, these effects can lower blood glucose immediately after exercise and for <strong>up to 24 hours</strong> afterward.²⁴</p>
            </div>
        </div>
    </div>
    <!-- 3.2. Getting started -->
    <div class="bg-white rounded-2xl shadow-lg border border-gray-100 p-8 mb-8">
        <div class="flex items-center mb-6">
            <div class="w-10 h-10 bg-gradient-to-br from-green-500 to-teal-600 rounded-lg flex items-center justify-center text-white font-bold text-lg mr-3">
                🎯
            </div>
            <h3 class="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900">3.2. Getting Started: A Weekly Schedule for a Beginner</h3>
        </div>
        <div class="prose prose-lg max-w-none text-gray-700 leading-relaxed space-y-6">
            <p>This plan starts with manageable sessions for someone with little previous exercise experience. The ADA and KDA recommend at least 150 minutes of moderate aerobic activity each week and strength training on at least two days.³</p>
            <div class="overflow-x-auto">
                <table class="w-full border-collapse border border-gray-300">
                    <thead>
                        <tr class="bg-gray-100">
                            <th class="border border-gray-300 p-3 text-left font-bold">Days</th>

                        </tr>

                        <tr class="bg-blue-50">

                        <tr class="bg-green-50">

                            <td class="border border-gray-300 p-3">Strength training</td>

            </div>
            <h3 class="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900">3.3. Finding the Right Intensity</h3>
        </div>
        <div class="prose prose-lg max-w-none text-gray-700 leading-relaxed space-y-6">
            <p>“Moderate intensity” can be hard to judge at first. These two methods can help you find a suitable pace.</p>
            <div class="grid md:grid-cols-2 gap-6">
                <div class="bg-gradient-to-br from-emerald-50 to-green-50 rounded-xl p-6 border border-emerald-200">
                    <h4 class="text-lg font-bold text-emerald-800 mb-3">💬 The talk test</h4>
                    <p class="text-emerald-700 text-sm mb-3">Try speaking to someone as you exercise. If conversation is completely effortless, you may be working at light intensity. If you are too breathless to get out more than a word or two, the intensity is too high for this session.</p>
                    <div class="bg-emerald-100 rounded-lg p-3 border border-emerald-300">
                        <p class="text-emerald-800 font-semibold text-sm">At moderate intensity, you can keep up a conversation but would find it hard to sing.²⁸</p>
                    </div>
                </div>
                <div class="bg-gradient-to-br from-blue-50 to-indigo-50 rounded-xl p-6 border border-blue-200">
                    <h4 class="text-lg font-bold text-blue-800 mb-3">💓 Target heart rate (an estimate)</h4>
                    <p class="text-blue-700 text-sm mb-3">A target heart rate is another way to estimate exercise intensity if you prefer a number.</p>
                    <div class="bg-blue-100 rounded-lg p-3 border border-blue-300 space-y-2">
                        <p class="text-blue-800 text-sm">• Estimated maximum: 220 − age = 170 beats per minute in this example</p>
                        <p class="text-blue-800 text-sm">• Moderate-intensity target: 85–119 beats per minute (50–70% of the estimated maximum)</p>
                        <p class="text-blue-700 text-xs">You can use a smartwatch or fitness band to monitor your heart rate.³³</p>
                    </div>

            <div class="bg-gradient-to-r from-indigo-100 to-purple-100 rounded-xl p-6 border border-indigo-200">

                    </div>
                    <div class="space-y-3">
                        <div class="flex items-center space-x-3">

        </div>
        <div class="prose prose-lg max-w-none text-gray-700 leading-relaxed space-y-6">
            <p>Self-monitoring of blood glucose (SMBG) shows how your body responds to a meal, activity or a stressful day. Recording the context alongside each reading can help you interpret it.³⁵</p>
            <div class="bg-gradient-to-r from-blue-100 to-indigo-100 rounded-xl p-6 border border-blue-200">
                <h4 class="text-lg font-bold text-blue-800 mb-3">💡 Another way to look at readings</h4>
                <p class="text-blue-700 italic">“Blood glucose readings are information you can use to make decisions. Each one gives you a chance to learn something about your own body.”</p>
            </div>
            <p>A structured series of readings can reveal patterns while you and your care team work on stabilizing glucose.³⁵</p>
            <div class="grid md:grid-cols-2 gap-6">
                <div class="bg-yellow-50 rounded-xl p-6 border border-yellow-200">
                    <h4 class="text-lg font-bold text-yellow-800 mb-4">📋 Measuring at different times</h4>
                    <div class="space-y-3 text-yellow-700">
                        <div class="flex items-center space-x-3">
                            <div class="w-6 h-6 bg-yellow-600 rounded-full flex items-center justify-center text-white text-xs font-bold">1</div>
                            <p class="text-sm"><strong>On waking, before breakfast:</strong> See how glucose changed overnight.</p>
                        </div>
                        <div class="flex items-center space-x-3">
                            <div class="w-6 h-6 bg-yellow-600 rounded-full flex items-center justify-center text-white text-xs font-bold">2</div>
                            <p class="text-sm"><strong>Before a meal:</strong> Provide a starting point for decisions about portions and prescribed medication dosing.</p>
                        </div>
                        <div class="flex items-center space-x-3">
                            <div class="w-6 h-6 bg-yellow-600 rounded-full flex items-center justify-center text-white text-xs font-bold">3</div>
                            <p class="text-sm"><strong>Two hours after starting a meal:</strong> See the glucose response to what you ate.</p>
                        </div>
                        <div class="flex items-center space-x-3">
                            <div class="w-6 h-6 bg-yellow-600 rounded-full flex items-center justify-center text-white text-xs font-bold">4</div>

                    <p class="text-purple-700 text-sm mb-3">Bring the record to appointments. You and your care team can look for patterns and decide whether to adjust meals, activity or medication.</p>

                <p class="text-red-700">Emotional stress releases hormones such as cortisol that can raise glucose.⁴³ Managing stress can support both emotional well-being and glucose management.</p>
            </div>

                <h4 class="text-lg font-bold text-blue-800 mb-4">🆘 A toolkit for stressful moments</h4>
                <p class="text-blue-700 mb-4">These are short exercises you can use as stress begins to build.</p>
                <div class="grid md:grid-cols-2 gap-4">
                    <div class="bg-white rounded-lg p-4 shadow-sm">
                        <h5 class="font-bold text-blue-800 mb-2">🌬️ 4–7–8 breathing</h5>
                        <p class="text-blue-700 text-sm">Sit comfortably and close your eyes. Breathe in through your nose for four seconds, hold for seven and breathe out slowly through your mouth for eight. Repeating the cycle three times may help you settle your breathing and feel calmer.⁴³</p>

                        <h5 class="font-bold text-blue-800 mb-2">🧘 One minute of mindfulness</h5>

                        <h5 class="font-bold text-blue-800 mb-2">📝 A gratitude journal</h5>
                        <p class="text-blue-700 text-sm">At the end of the day, write down one thing you appreciated. Recalling positive experiences can help ease stress.⁴³</p>
                    </div>
                    <div class="bg-white rounded-lg p-4 shadow-sm">
                        <h5 class="font-bold text-blue-800 mb-2">👨‍👩‍👧‍👦 Build a support network</h5>
                        <p class="text-blue-700 text-sm">Share your goals and the things you find difficult with family members. Let them know what kind of encouragement would help.²²</p>
                    </div>
                </div>

                        <th class="border border-gray-300 p-4 text-left font-bold">Initial management target</th>
                        <th class="border border-gray-300 p-4 text-left font-bold">Notes</th>
                    </tr>
                </thead>
                <tbody>
                    <tr class="bg-red-50">
                        <td class="border border-gray-300 p-4 font-semibold">Glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c)</td>
                        <td class="border border-gray-300 p-4 text-center font-bold text-red-600">&lt; 7.0%</td>
                        <td class="border border-gray-300 p-4">HbA1c reflects average glucose over the past two to three months. Maintaining it below 7.0% is associated with a lower risk of complications in many people with diabetes.²</td>
                    </tr>
                    <tr class="bg-blue-50">
                        <td class="border border-gray-300 p-4 font-semibold">Fasting blood glucose</td>
                        <td class="border border-gray-300 p-4 text-center font-bold text-blue-600">80 ~ 130 mg/dL</td>
                        <td class="border border-gray-300 p-4">Measured before breakfast after fasting for at least eight hours. It provides information about overnight glucose production by the liver and the body’s insulin response.⁴⁴</td>
                    </tr>
                    <tr class="bg-green-50">
                        <td class="border border-gray-300 p-4 font-semibold">Two-hour post-meal glucose</td>
                        <td class="border border-gray-300 p-4 text-center font-bold text-green-600">&lt; 180 mg/dL</td>
                        <td class="border border-gray-300 p-4">Measure two hours after the start of the meal to assess the glucose response to food and insulin.⁴⁴</td>
                    </tr>
                    <tr class="bg-purple-50">
                        <td class="border border-gray-300 p-4 font-semibold">Blood pressure</td>
                        <td class="border border-gray-300 p-4 text-center font-bold text-purple-600">&lt; 130/80 mmHg</td>
                        <td class="border border-gray-300 p-4">Diabetes increases cardiovascular risk. The example uses a tighter blood pressure target than the 140/90 mmHg threshold used for comparison in the guide.³</td>
                    </tr>
                    <tr class="bg-orange-50">
                        <td class="border border-gray-300 p-4 font-semibold">LDL cholesterol (“bad” cholesterol)</td>
                        <td class="border border-gray-300 p-4 text-center font-bold text-orange-600">&lt; 100 mg/dL</td>
                        <td class="border border-gray-300 p-4">LDL contributes to plaque buildup in arteries. A subsequent risk assessment could support a lower target of less than 70 mg/dL.³</td>

            <p class="text-amber-700">These values can change with health status, age and other conditions. What matters is working steadily toward goals agreed on with your own care team.</p>

<!-- Chapter 6: Special situations and emergencies -->
<div class="space-y-8">
    <div class="flex items-center mb-8">
        <div class="w-12 h-12 bg-gradient-to-br from-red-500 to-rose-600 rounded-xl flex items-center justify-center text-white font-bold text-xl mr-4">
            🚨
        </div>
        <h2 class="text-3xl font-bold text-gray-900">6. Special Situations and Emergencies</h2>
    </div>
    <div class="bg-white rounded-2xl shadow-lg border border-gray-100 p-8 mb-8">
        <div class="prose prose-lg max-w-none text-gray-700 leading-relaxed space-y-6">
            <p>
                Low blood glucose and illness can interrupt an otherwise familiar routine. Knowing what to do ahead of time can help you respond safely and with more confidence.
            </p>
        </div>
    </div>
    <!-- 6.1. Low blood glucose -->
    <div class="bg-white rounded-2xl shadow-lg border border-gray-100 p-8 mb-8">
        <div class="flex items-center mb-6">
            <div class="w-10 h-10 bg-gradient-to-br from-red-500 to-pink-600 rounded-lg flex items-center justify-center text-white font-bold text-lg mr-3">
                🩸
            </div>
            <h3 class="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900">6.1. Low Blood Glucose: The 15–15 Rule</h3>
        </div>
        <div class="prose prose-lg max-w-none text-gray-700 leading-relaxed space-y-6">
            <p>Low blood glucose (below 70 mg/dL) needs prompt treatment. If the person is awake and can swallow safely, follow the <strong>15–15 rule</strong>.⁴⁸</p>
            <div class="grid md:grid-cols-2 gap-6">
                <div class="space-y-4">
                    <div class="bg-red-50 rounded-xl p-6 border border-red-200">
                        <h4 class="text-lg font-bold text-red-800 mb-3">1️⃣ Recognize the symptoms</h4>
                        <p class="text-red-700 text-sm">Sudden sweating, trembling, intense hunger, dizziness, a racing heart, anxiety or blurred vision can indicate low blood glucose.⁴⁸</p>
                    </div>
                    <div class="bg-orange-50 rounded-xl p-6 border border-orange-200">
                        <h4 class="text-lg font-bold text-orange-800 mb-3">2️⃣ Check your glucose</h4>
                        <p class="text-orange-700 text-sm">If possible, check immediately. A reading below 70 mg/dL calls for the next step. If you cannot check and have symptoms that suggest a low, treat the suspected low promptly.</p>
                    </div>
                </div>
                <div class="space-y-4">
                    <div class="bg-yellow-50 rounded-xl p-6 border border-yellow-200">
                        <h4 class="text-lg font-bold text-yellow-800 mb-3">3️⃣ Take 15 g of fast-acting carbohydrate (the first 15)</h4>
                        <p class="text-yellow-700 text-sm mb-3">The guide gives 15–20 g of simple carbohydrate as a treatment range. Fat or protein can delay absorption, so chocolate and ice cream are poor choices for treating a low.</p>
                        <div class="bg-yellow-100 rounded-lg p-3 border border-yellow-300">
                            <h5 class="font-bold text-yellow-800 text-sm mb-2">Examples of fast-acting carbohydrate:</h5>
                            <ul class="text-yellow-700 text-xs space-y-1">
                                <li>• ½ cup fruit juice (about 100–125 mL)</li>
                                <li>• ½ cup regular cola or lemon-lime soda</li>
                                <li>• 3–4 pieces of candy</li>
                                <li>• 1 tablespoon of honey or sugar</li>
                                <li>• Commercial glucose tablets, candy or gel</li>
                            </ul>
                        </div>
                    </div>
                    <div class="bg-green-50 rounded-xl p-6 border border-green-200">
                        <h4 class="text-lg font-bold text-green-800 mb-3">4️⃣ Wait 15 minutes and recheck (the second 15)</h4>
                        <p class="text-green-700 text-sm">Rest for 15 minutes, then recheck. If the reading is still below 70 mg/dL, repeat step 3. Once glucose has recovered, if the next meal is more than an hour away, have a snack containing some protein and complex carbohydrate, such as crackers with cheese or half a slice of bread with a protein source.⁴⁸</p>
                    </div>
                </div>
            </div>
        </div>
    </div>
    <!-- 6.2. Sick days -->
    <div class="bg-white rounded-2xl shadow-lg border border-gray-100 p-8 mb-8">
        <div class="flex items-center mb-6">
            <div class="w-10 h-10 bg-gradient-to-br from-blue-500 to-indigo-600 rounded-lg flex items-center justify-center text-white font-bold text-lg mr-3">
                🤒
            </div>
            <h3 class="text-2xl font-bold text-gray-900">6.2. Sick-Day Guidance</h3>
        </div>
        <div class="prose prose-lg max-w-none text-gray-700 leading-relaxed space-y-6">
            <p>A cold, a flu-like illness or gastroenteritis can cause blood glucose to rise or fall unpredictably. The following points can help you prepare a sick-day plan with your clinician.⁵¹</p>
            <div class="grid md:grid-cols-2 gap-6">
                <div class="space-y-4">
                    <div class="bg-red-50 rounded-xl p-6 border border-red-200">
                        <h4 class="text-lg font-bold text-red-800 mb-3">📋 1. Follow your clinician’s sick-day medication plan</h4>
                        <p class="text-red-700 text-sm">Ask which medicines or insulin doses to continue or adjust if you are ill or cannot eat. Follow your clinician’s instructions and contact the care team if you are unsure. The body’s stress response can raise blood glucose even when you are not eating.⁵¹</p>
                    </div>
                    <div class="bg-blue-50 rounded-xl p-6 border border-blue-200">
                        <h4 class="text-lg font-bold text-blue-800 mb-3">📊 2. Check glucose more often</h4>
                        <p class="text-blue-700 text-sm">Illness can cause larger swings in blood glucose. The example plan calls for checks at least every three to four hours to track changes.⁵¹</p>
                    </div>
                </div>
                <div class="space-y-4">
                    <div class="bg-cyan-50 rounded-xl p-6 border border-cyan-200">
                        <h4 class="text-lg font-bold text-cyan-800 mb-3">💧 3. Keep up your fluid intake</h4>
                        <p class="text-cyan-700 text-sm">Dehydration can worsen high blood glucose. Try to take fluids such as water or barley tea every hour. If you cannot eat solid food, your sick-day plan may include carbohydrate-containing liquids such as juice or sports drinks to help prevent low glucose, and sugar-free fluids such as water or clear broth when glucose is higher.⁵¹</p>
                    </div>
                    <div class="bg-orange-50 rounded-xl p-6 border border-orange-200">
                        <h4 class="text-lg font-bold text-orange-800 mb-3">🚨 4. Know when to seek medical help</h4>
                        <div class="text-orange-700 text-sm space-y-2">
                            <p>Contact your care team promptly in any of the following situations:</p>
                            <ul class="space-y-1 ml-4">
                                <li>• Vomiting or diarrhea continues and you cannot keep fluids down.</li>
                                <li>• A fever of 38.5°C or higher persists.</li>
                                <li>• Two consecutive glucose readings are 250 mg/dL or higher.</li>
                                <li>• You have repeated episodes of low blood glucose.</li>
                                <li>• You are unsure what to do.</li>
                            </ul>
                        </div>
                    </div>
                </div>
            </div>
            <div class="bg-emerald-50 rounded-xl p-6 border border-emerald-200">
                <h4 class="text-lg font-bold text-emerald-800 mb-3">💪 Building confidence</h4>
                <p class="text-emerald-700">Knowing your response plan in advance can help you act with confidence when something unexpected happens.</p>
