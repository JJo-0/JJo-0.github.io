> **Study stage.** A 76-week phase 3 randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in 752 adults. This article discusses an investigational drug and the interpretation of trial results.

**The result in one sentence.** At 76 weeks, mean body weight changed by −9.8% in the 6 mg group and −3.9% with placebo. The company separately reported weight loss of up to 13.1% under a different analysis. Interpreting the results also requires separating gastrointestinal adverse events from treatment discontinuations.

## Why are there two numbers?

Survodutide is a dual agonist that stimulates the glucagon and GLP-1 receptors. A receptor is a protein that receives a signal and changes how a cell responds. Acting on two pathways provides a rationale for testing the drug. It does not establish superiority over existing drugs.

SYNCHRONIZE-2 randomly assigned adults with a body mass index (BMI) of at least 27 and type 2 diabetes to 3.6 mg, 6.0 mg, or placebo in a 1:1:1 ratio. Participants and investigators were blinded to the allocation. The trial compared outcomes over 76 weeks of once-weekly subcutaneous administration. [Methods in the article abstract.]

Distinguish assignment from receiving at least one dose. A published baseline paper for the same trial reported 755 randomized and 752 treated. Three people who did not meet eligibility criteria were randomized in error and excluded from statistical analysis. They were not three participants who simply discontinued during follow-up. The final NEJM abstract reports 752 participants, with 250, 251, and 251 in the three groups. The full final flow diagram was outside this review. [Baseline paper, Section 3.1, PDF p. 4; final paper abstract; registry NCT06066528.]

The treatment-regimen estimand highlighted by NEJM includes outcomes in the assigned group after discontinuation, temporary interruption, or use of another obesity treatment.

Mean changes in body weight in that analysis were:

- 3.6 mg: **−8.2%** (95% CI −9.2% to −7.2%)
- 6.0 mg: **−9.8%** (95% CI −10.8% to −8.8%)
- Placebo: **−3.9%** (95% CI −4.9% to −2.9%)

These estimates use the treatment-regimen definition above.

The confidence intervals describe uncertainty about each group’s mean. Individual participants’ weight changes need not fall in those ranges. An interval for a difference between groups is a separate calculation. Subtracting endpoints of the two group intervals cannot produce the interval for the treatment effect. [Results in the publicly available NEJM abstract, checked October 22, 2026.]

In a separate efficacy estimand, the company reported weight loss of up to 13.1% versus 3.1% with placebo. The public announcement reviewed did not fully specify assumptions for missing values or how post-discontinuation data entered this analysis. It cannot simply be described as the observed mean for participants who took the drug to the end.

The analyses ask different questions. An estimand specifies whose outcome is of interest, which treatment circumstances count, and what effect is being estimated. The full statistical methods and supplement were not available to this review. The difference between estimates cannot be attributed entirely to adverse events.

Keep the denominator straight even within the treatment-regimen analysis. The −9.8% in the 6 mg group is relative to baseline weight. Subtracting the placebo mean of −3.9% gives a descriptive difference of −5.9 percentage points. This arithmetic is neither an adjusted treatment effect from the full statistical model nor a new significance test.

## How many participants lost at least 5%?

Mean weight change and the proportion crossing a threshold are different outcomes. The −9.8% is the group’s mean change, whereas 64.5% below is the fraction of participants reaching at least 5% weight loss. The numerators and denominators differ.

Under the treatment-regimen estimand, the proportions losing at least 5% were **57.6%** with 3.6 mg, **64.5%** with 6.0 mg, and <strong>35.1%</strong> with placebo.

Baseline HbA1c was 7.4%. Changes were approximately −0.9, −0.8, and −0.2 percentage points, respectively. This glycemic endpoint is separate from body weight.

The trial thus recorded changes in a marker of glucose control alongside changes in weight. These measurements alone cannot establish fewer long-term complications.

## Tolerability alongside efficacy

Gastrointestinal (GI) adverse events occurred in 182 of 250 participants (72.8%) at 3.6 mg, 195 of 251 (77.7%) at 6.0 mg, and 97 of 251 (38.6%) on placebo. [Results in the paper abstract.]

The events were generally described as mild to moderate and transient. Experiencing an event and stopping treatment because of it are different outcomes. The percentages above are event rates. Tolerability matters in a long-term treatment because it affects whether people can continue.

The company separately reported discontinuation because of GI events in 18% of participants receiving the study drug and 1.2% receiving placebo. The statement reviewed did not give a dose-specific table of counts and denominators. It therefore cannot support calculating discontinuation counts for each dose group.

The ability to stay on treatment is important, but subtracting 9.8% from 13.1% cannot quantify weight loss forgone because of GI effects. The underlying questions, observations, handling of discontinuation, and missing-data assumptions may all differ. Resolving them requires the full paper and statistical analysis plan.

## How does it compare with other obesity drugs?

The trial cannot answer that on its own.

The comparator was placebo. It did not include semaglutide or tirzepatide. Comparing percentages across trials without matching participant characteristics, dose escalation, estimands, and dropout handling can give a misleading ranking.

The reported weight and HbA1c results do not establish reductions in heart attack, stroke, or death.

## What could change in practice?

The largest reported weight-loss percentage is unlikely to be enough to distinguish obesity drugs.

Future comparisons should also examine dose escalation, discontinuation, retention of muscle mass, long-term cardiovascular, kidney, and liver outcomes, and weight maintenance. Each needs evidence separate from the abstract’s weight endpoint.

Survodutide adds phase 3 weight data for a dual agonist. This trial does not isolate how much each receptor mechanism contributed to GI adverse events. Compare measured efficacy and safety under comparable conditions.

## What to watch next

Look for regulatory submissions and decisions, discontinuation under flexible dose escalation, cardiovascular outcomes, and head-to-head trials. Whether weight loss and treatment persistence continue beyond 76 weeks also remains open.

**Check your understanding.** Is the 77.7% who experienced a GI event also the percentage who discontinued because of one? These are different events. Can two weight-loss estimates be subtracted to calculate the effect lost to side effects? Differences in estimands and handling of observations prevent that interpretation.
