## Why does the size of a printing plate matter for advanced chips?

Printing a book requires a master containing the text and paper to receive its image. A similar distinction matters in semiconductor lithography.

A <strong>wafer</strong> is the silicon substrate on which chips are fabricated. A <strong>photomask</strong> is the master that carries the circuit pattern. EUV uses a reflective mask and mirrors, so a projector shining light through transparent film is an imperfect analogy. What matters here is which object carries the original and which receives the image. [ASML on lithography optics](https://www.asml.com/en/technology/lithography-principles/lenses-and-mirrors)

A report about a “12-inch mask” therefore describes the size of the patterned master. It announces no change to the silicon wafer diameter.

## DRAM holds the information a computer is working on

**DRAM** is the working memory used to handle active programs and data quickly. It stores information as charge and refreshes it periodically. Long-term storage serves a different purpose and can retain information with power off. [Samsung’s introduction to DRAM](https://semiconductor.samsung.com/dram/)

Think of a desk and a bookshelf. Files for long-term storage go on the shelf, while the documents you are using lie open on the desk. DRAM plays the role of the desk. The capacity of that desk, or a process improvement in its manufacture, is only one influence on system speed.

## Two plans in the joint announcement

On September 8, Samsung Electronics and ASML announced an expansion of their High-NA EUV collaboration. Samsung said it would **help prepare the photomask ecosystem for a move from 6-inch to 12-inch masks** and **aim to introduce High-NA EUV into volume production of future DRAM by 2028**.

The companies expect a larger mask to ease stitching constraints and help improve productivity and costs. The announcement gives objectives. Measured cost reductions and volume-production results would require manufacturing data. [ASML announcement](https://www.asml.com/en/news/press-releases/2026/samsung-electronics-and-asml-expand-collaboration-on-next-generation-semiconductor-manufacturing) · [Samsung Electronics announcement](https://news.samsung.com/global/samsung-electronics-and-asml-expand-strategic-collaboration-for-next-generation-semiconductor-manufacturing)

The two websites report the same collaboration. The releases document what the partners intend to do and count as one announcement, rather than two independent performance evaluations.

## How are sharper optics and a larger mask related?

Resolving smaller details is a different problem from efficiently exposing a large area. A printer might render each letter sharply yet have to divide a large drawing into several passes if its print field becomes smaller.

Anyone who has tiled a large picture across several sheets knows the difficulty. Even a small displacement at the seam makes a line look broken. On a chip, such a seam can affect an actual electrical connection.

Joining patterns printed in separate fields is called <strong>stitching</strong>. Expanding the mask format is one way the partners hope to address both stitching constraints and productivity as they introduce High-NA EUV. [Goals of the collaboration](https://www.asml.com/en/news/press-releases/2026/samsung-electronics-and-asml-expand-collaboration-on-next-generation-semiconductor-manufacturing)

A larger plate does not yield a fixed multiplier for throughput. Exposure time, stage motion and alignment, inspection, and equipment uptime all affect the production rate. Mask dimensions alone do not determine fab output.

## What a fab must change beyond purchasing a tool

Imagine a new printer whose paper does not fit its feeder. The inspection system cannot read its prints, and the operator’s software only supports the old format.

A mask-format change has similar dependencies. Manufacturing the master, inspecting it for defects, protecting and handling it, and transporting and aligning it in the tool all have to work together. These are transition requirements, not findings that any supplier has fallen short.

Large manufacturers already run many interconnected process steps. An improvement in precision at one step brings limited productivity gains if neighboring steps cannot accommodate it. Joint development and standards announcements matter because **they identify both the technical objective and the participants preparing for it**.

## How to read the 2028 target

A plan to introduce a process by 2028 sets a future milestone. It establishes neither current volume production nor a common conversion date for every DRAM product.

The announcement leaves open which products will use it, how many tools will be deployed, output, yield, and cost per chip. Filling those gaps with assumptions would turn a plan into an apparent result.

**Which product and layer use the process? Is there a part shipping to customers? Do the schedule and cost advantages hold?** Answers to those questions would constitute the next manufacturing milestone.

## Reading this alongside imec’s announcement

imec asks whether a material and a process can produce a particular fine pattern. Samsung and ASML ask how new equipment and a new mask format can enter a production system.

The announcements illuminate the same transition from different positions. They do not establish that Samsung adopted any specific resist or process from the imec announcement.

What Samsung disclosed was **a plan to adapt masks and the manufacturing ecosystem to more precise exposure equipment**. Completion of a new DRAM product is a separate milestone.
