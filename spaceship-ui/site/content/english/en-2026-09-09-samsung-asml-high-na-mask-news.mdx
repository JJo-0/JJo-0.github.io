## Why does the size of a printing plate matter for advanced chips?

Printing a book requires a master containing the text and paper to receive its image. A similar distinction matters in semiconductor lithography.

A <strong>wafer</strong> is the silicon substrate on which chips are fabricated. A <strong>photomask</strong> is the master that carries the circuit pattern. EUV uses a reflective mask and mirrors, so a projector shining light through transparent film is an imperfect analogy. What matters here is which object carries the original and which receives the image. [ASML on the EUV reticle](https://www.asml.com/en/products/euv-lithography-systems)

A report about a “12-inch mask” therefore describes the size of the patterned master. It announces no change to the silicon wafer diameter.

### A mask transfers the pattern repeatedly

The wafer receives the pattern. The mask carries a master pattern that must be written, inspected, stored, handled, and delivered to the exposure tool for reuse. Increasing its size therefore changes the requirements on the surrounding equipment and procedures.

An EUV mask reflects light. A diagram should show the reflected light passing through the projection optics to form an image in the wafer resist. A transparent slide would suggest the wrong optical path. The diagram need not assume a mirror count or product dimensions without a corresponding technical specification. [ASML's description of the EUV reticle](https://www.asml.com/en/products/euv-lithography-systems)

## DRAM holds the information a computer is working on

**DRAM** is the working memory used to handle active programs and data quickly. It stores information as charge and refreshes it periodically. Long-term storage serves a different purpose and can retain information with power off. [Samsung’s introduction to DRAM](https://semiconductor.samsung.com/dram/)

Think of a desk and a bookshelf. Files for long-term storage go on the shelf, while the documents you are using lie open on the desk. DRAM plays the role of the desk. The capacity of that desk, or a process improvement in its manufacture, is only one influence on system speed.

### Cell size and finished DRAM capacity

A DRAM cell stores charge and provides a way to access it. Denser cells may hold more information per unit area, but a finished device also needs addressing, read/write circuitry, and interconnects. A percentage reduction in cell area alone cannot establish a percentage gain in product capacity.

Stored charge changes over time, so the memory periodically refreshes it. Scaling has to preserve the ability to distinguish states and retain data reliably. Better lithography contributes, together with circuit design, device behavior, and manufacturing variation.

Growing AI memory demand helps explain interest in the collaboration. It does not specify a particular DRAM product's shipment date or volume. Industry demand and demonstrated adoption of a specific process require separate evidence.

## Two plans in the joint announcement

On September 8, Samsung Electronics and ASML announced an expansion of their High-NA EUV collaboration. Samsung said it would **help prepare the photomask ecosystem for a move from 6-inch to 12-inch masks** and **aim to introduce High-NA EUV into volume production of future DRAM by 2028**.

The companies expect a larger mask to ease stitching constraints and help improve productivity and costs. The announcement gives objectives. Measured cost reductions and volume-production results would require manufacturing data. [ASML announcement](https://www.asml.com/en/news/press-releases/2026/samsung-electronics-and-asml-expand-collaboration-on-next-generation-semiconductor-manufacturing) · [Samsung Electronics announcement](https://news.samsung.com/global/samsung-electronics-and-asml-expand-strategic-collaboration-for-next-generation-semiconductor-manufacturing)

The two websites report the same collaboration. The releases document what the partners intend to do and count as one announcement, rather than two independent performance evaluations.

### Tool introduction and preparation of the larger format have separate timelines

A manufacturer can introduce High-NA optics using the existing mask format and later expand to a larger format. The stated 2028 goal for introducing the technology into DRAM manufacturing does not set a completion date for every part of the large-mask ecosystem.

The joint statement describes intentions and expectations, and its forward-looking statement identifies uncertainty about future outcomes. Development collaboration, tool commissioning, customer product qualification, and stable volume production are distinct milestones. [Joint announcement](https://www.asml.com/en/news/press-releases/2026/samsung-electronics-and-asml-expand-collaboration-on-next-generation-semiconductor-manufacturing)

## How are sharper optics and a larger mask related?

Resolving smaller details is a different problem from efficiently exposing a large area. A printer might render each letter sharply yet have to divide a large drawing into several passes if its print field becomes smaller.

Anyone who has tiled a large picture across several sheets knows the difficulty. Even a small displacement at the seam makes a line look broken. On a chip, such a seam can affect an actual electrical connection.

Joining patterns printed in separate fields is called <strong>stitching</strong>. Expanding the mask format is one way the partners hope to address both stitching constraints and productivity as they introduce High-NA EUV. [Goals of the collaboration](https://www.asml.com/en/news/press-releases/2026/samsung-electronics-and-asml-expand-collaboration-on-next-generation-semiconductor-manufacturing)

A larger plate does not yield a fixed multiplier for throughput. Exposure time, stage motion and alignment, inspection, and equipment uptime all affect the production rate. Mask dimensions alone do not determine fab output.

### Resolving fine lines and covering a large field

Resolution measures the ability to distinguish two nearby features. Exposure field describes the area imaged in one shot. A finer brush does not enlarge the paper, and larger paper does not sharpen the brush.

Image reduction in the optics, mask dimensions, and stage movements all affect usable field and throughput. A larger format may ease a field constraint. Whether a circuit needs multiple fields and how much it benefits depend on product dimensions and layout.

### Stitching across fields and aligning successive layers

Stitching joins the boundaries of separately exposed fields. Overlay concerns alignment between different circuit layers. Reducing the need to stitch part of a design still leaves layer-to-layer alignment requirements.

The master pattern must be written accurately, and the exposure tool must transfer it to the intended position repeatedly. Distortion, handling, inspection, and calibration all matter alongside mask dimensions. The announcement leaves quantitative qualification of these items to later evidence.

## What a fab must change beyond purchasing a tool

Imagine a new printer whose paper does not fit its feeder. The inspection system cannot read its prints, and the operator’s software only supports the old format.

A mask-format change has similar dependencies. Manufacturing the master, inspecting it for defects, protecting and handling it, and transporting and aligning it in the tool all have to work together. These are transition requirements, not findings that any supplier has fallen short.

Large manufacturers already run many interconnected process steps. An improvement in precision at one step brings limited productivity gains if neighboring steps cannot accommodate it. Joint development and standards announcements matter because **they identify both the technical objective and the participants preparing for it**.

### Throughput, availability, and yield

Throughput measures output per unit time, availability measures the fraction of required time for which a tool is usable, and yield measures conformance to a product's quality criteria. A faster exposure produces fewer extra good dies than expected if the tool stops frequently or more output fails inspection.

A cost comparison therefore includes inspection, mask changes, rework, consumables, and downtime. Lower cost is a reasonable development goal. Measuring a reduction requires comparable products and quality requirements.

### A supply chain needs compatible specifications and qualification

Mask production, pattern writing, defect inspection, transport, and storage have to support the same format. An equipment supplier's presence in a relevant business does not establish that it has received an order. Industry direction, signed contracts, and realized revenue are separate observations.

Look for agreed specifications, interoperable prototypes, and evaluation in a customer's process. Evidence that several stages work together is more informative than a publicity photograph from one vendor. The announcement identifies parties that intend to do this work.

## How to read the 2028 target

A plan to introduce a process by 2028 sets a future milestone. It establishes neither current volume production nor a common conversion date for every DRAM product.

The announcement leaves open which products will use it, how many tools will be deployed, output, yield, and cost per chip. Filling those gaps with assumptions would turn a plan into an apparent result.

**Which product and layer use the process? Is there a part shipping to customers? Do the schedule and cost advantages hold?** Answers to those questions would constitute the next manufacturing milestone.

### How a defect in the master can recur on the wafer

A defect in a master pattern or its surface can affect successive exposures. Mask writing, inspection, storage, and handling are therefore linked. A larger format requires precise handling as well as space for more pattern area. The collaboration announcement gives no specific mask inspection yield to use in a calculation.

Mask lifetime and replacement time also contribute to cost. A larger exposure field alone cannot establish a proportional gain in total fab output. Available tool time, inspection, maintenance, and scrapped products enter the cost per good die.

Participation by a customer and its supplier makes the announcement informative about process requirements. A shared target is still distinct from demonstrated completion of the engineering work.

### What would count as the next milestone?

Published mask specifications, test hardware, and results from qualification on a customer's process would make the plan easier to assess. Especially useful would be comparable throughput and processing steps at the same quality level. An image covering a larger field cannot establish a specific percentage cost reduction.

For individual suppliers, track product adoption, confirmed specifications, actual orders, and manufacturing results in sequence. Converting a possible category of equipment into a company's forecast revenue combines several assumptions. The public announcements do not disclose those contracts.
