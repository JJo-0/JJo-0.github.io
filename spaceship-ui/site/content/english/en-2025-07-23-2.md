        <h2 class="text-3xl font-bold text-gray-900">Foundations of omega-3 quality</h2>
    </div>

<div class="bg-white rounded-2xl shadow-lg border border-gray-100 p-8">
    <p class="text-gray-700 leading-relaxed mb-6">
        Understand molecular form and ingredient source when considering absorption, purity, and quality. Neither factor alone determines clinical outcomes or safety.
    </p>

<!-- 1.1 Molecular forms -->
<div class="mb-8">
    <h3 class="text-2xl font-semibold text-gray-900 mb-6 flex items-center">
        <div class="w-8 h-8 bg-blue-100 rounded-lg flex items-center justify-center mr-3">

            </svg>
        </div>
        1.1 Molecular forms: The source’s case for rTG
    </h3>
    <p class="text-gray-700 leading-relaxed mb-6">
        The source groups triglyceride (TG), ethyl ester (EE), and re-esterified triglyceride (rTG) forms into three generations, describing developments in concentration and absorption. This is its explanatory framing, not a universal clinical ranking.
    </p>

<div class="space-y-6">
    <!-- TG -->
    <div class="bg-orange-50 rounded-xl p-6 border border-orange-200">
        <h4 class="text-lg font-semibold text-gray-900 mb-3 flex items-center">
            <div class="w-6 h-6 bg-orange-200 rounded flex items-center justify-center mr-2">
                1
            </div>
            Generation 1 in the source: TG (triglyceride)
        </h4>
        <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed mb-3">
            A glycerol backbone holds three fatty acids in the natural triglyceride structure of fish oil. Digestion and bioavailability depend on formulation and food conditions.
        </p>
        <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm">
            The source describes natural oil around 30% EPA/DHA and simplifies this as one omega-3 fatty acid among three. Actual triglycerides have variable fatty-acid compositions; it is not a rule that every molecule has exactly one EPA/DHA and two unnecessary saturated fats.
        </p>
    </div>

<!-- EE -->
<div class="bg-yellow-50 rounded-xl p-6 border border-yellow-200">
    <h4 class="text-lg font-semibold text-gray-900 mb-3 flex items-center">
        <div class="w-6 h-6 bg-yellow-200 rounded flex items-center justify-center mr-2">
            2
        </div>
        Generation 2 in the source: EE (ethyl ester)
    </h4>
    <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed mb-3">
        Processing can replace the glycerol linkage with ethyl esters and concentrate EPA/DHA; the original cites concentrations from 50% to 90%. Check product-specific composition.
    </p>
    <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm">
        EE absorption can be lower under some conditions, especially low-fat intake, but calling it unfamiliar and inefficient does not make it clinically ineffective. The original gives prescription Omacor as an EE example; prescription products are distinct from supplements.
    </p>
</div>

<!-- rTG -->
<div class="bg-green-50 rounded-xl p-6 border border-green-200">
    <h4 class="text-lg font-semibold text-green-900 mb-3 flex items-center">
        <div class="w-6 h-6 bg-green-200 rounded flex items-center justify-center mr-2">
            3
        </div>
        Generation 3 in the source: rTG (re-esterified triglyceride)
    </h4>
    <p class="text-green-800 text-sm leading-relaxed mb-3">
        Concentrated fatty acids can be re-esterified onto glycerol, combining high EPA/DHA concentration with triglyceride structure. The original presents this as combining TG/EE advantages.
    </p>
    <p class="text-green-700 text-sm">
        The original cites a Copenhagen comparison with rTG 124, TG 100, and EE 73. These are relative bioavailability values with TG as baseline, not percentages of an ingested dose exceeding 100% absorption. Study conditions limit generalization.
    </p>
</div>
</div>

<div class="bg-gradient-to-r from-blue-50 to-indigo-50 rounded-lg p-6 mt-6 border border-blue-200">
    <h4 class="font-medium text-blue-900 mb-2">Original assessment:</h4>
    <p class="text-blue-800 text-sm leading-relaxed">
        The source argues for absorbed EPA/DHA rather than label amount alone and calls rTG its preferred choice despite cheaper EE options. Its claim that rTG is the only correct choice is an overstatement: formulation, meals, indication, evidence, and cost also matter.
    </p>
</div>

    </h3>
    <p class="text-gray-700 leading-relaxed mb-6">
        Oil quality and oxidation management matter for supplement quality and palatability, especially for those sensitive to fishy taste.
    </p>

<div class="space-y-6">
    <!-- Purity -->
    <div class="bg-white rounded-lg p-6">
        <h4 class="text-lg font-semibold text-gray-900 mb-3">Purity: Checking IFOS claims</h4>
        <p class="text-gray-700 text-sm leading-relaxed mb-4">
            Metals, dioxins, and PCBs are not visible to consumers. The original favors independent certification such as <strong>IFOS (International Fish Oil Standards)</strong> for labeled content, contaminants, and oxidation. It calls 5-Star the highest assurance; verify the current program, product, and batch. Ingredient certification must not be conflated with finished-product certification or a blanket safety guarantee.
        </p>

<!-- Freshness -->
<div class="bg-white rounded-lg p-6">
    <h4 class="text-lg font-semibold text-gray-900 mb-3">Freshness: TOTOX and aftertaste</h4>
    <p class="text-orange-800 text-sm leading-relaxed mb-4">
        Unsaturated oils oxidize with oxygen, light, and heat, potentially degrading quality and changing smell/taste. The source states oxidized oils are ineffective/harmful; real clinical effects depend on exposure and evidence, so avoid that categorical inference.
    </p>
    <p class="text-orange-800 text-sm leading-relaxed mb-4">
        TOTOX combines peroxide value, reflecting primary oxidation, and anisidine value, reflecting secondary oxidation. Lower values generally indicate less measured oxidation under comparable test conditions.
    </p>
