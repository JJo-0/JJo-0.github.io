## From nanometer wiring to system energy efficiency

imec presented 150 nm junctions, 30 nm interconnects, and a multilayer structure for NbTiN superconducting circuits. Its release and conference presentation describe how junction density could scale. <strong>The announcement establishes progress in fabrication. Processor speed, power consumption, and manufacturing yield still require measurements on a complete processor.</strong> [Official announcement][primary]

### A manufacturing platform for superconducting circuits

NbTiN contains niobium, titanium and nitrogen. Here the choice of material supports an attempt to make smaller junctions and interconnects than those in commonly studied superconducting technologies. At the 2026 Applied Superconductivity Conference, imec presented the work as a process foundation for scaling these circuits.[primary]

The announcement describes NbTiN junctions with amorphous-silicon barriers, three metal interconnect layers, 150 nm junction diameters and 30 nm wiring. It reports a density of 3.8 million junctions per square centimeter. Translating that density into processor throughput would require a circuit architecture and measured operation.[primary]

The images provide evidence of fabricated structures. Potential computing efficiency gains are system targets. A measured comparison with a GPU would need specified workloads, devices and system boundaries.

## Why track power when resistance falls to zero?

Under the right conditions, a superconductor can carry direct current with zero resistance. A circuit must also switch states, transmit signals, connect to external inputs and outputs, and stay cold. Small losses in a device tell us little on their own about power drawn from the wall.

One building block of a superconducting digital circuit is the Josephson junction. A thin barrier separates two superconductors, and the current depends on the phase difference between them. Scaling the circuit requires narrower wiring and reliable junction fabrication.

### From the material state to operation of a whole device

Superconductivity occurs within ranges of temperature, current and magnetic field. Zero resistance describes electrical behavior in that state. Maintaining it also requires the refrigerator to remove heat entering through the surroundings and signal connections.

An electronic circuit stores values, changes states and transmits signals. Even if interconnect losses fall, switching, control and input/output may consume energy. Specify whether an efficiency estimate covers a wire, a junction, a logic circuit or the entire refrigerated system.

A Josephson junction couples superconducting states across a thin barrier. The barrier material and area affect its current characteristics. Repeatedly fabricating a small structure with controlled properties therefore matters to circuit behavior.

## Reading the two junction equations

The ideal Josephson relations can be written as follows.

<Math display tex={String.raw`I=I_c\sin\phi,\qquad V=\frac{\hbar}{2e}\frac{d\phi}{dt}`} label="Josephson relations between phase difference and supercurrent, and between the phase derivative and voltage" />

Here I_c is the critical current, φ is the phase difference between the two superconductors, and V is voltage. Phase is a dimensionless angle measured in radians. The voltage depends on how fast the phase changes. A real junction also has resistance, capacitance, and thermal noise, so modeling it requires more than these two relations.

For a pulse in which the phase changes by 2π, integrating the voltage gives

<Math display tex={String.raw`\int V(t)\,dt=\frac{h}{2e}=\Phi_0`} label="Voltage integrated over a single 2π change in phase" />

The unit is volt-seconds (V·s), also the unit of magnetic flux. To obtain the energy per operation, one would integrate the product of voltage and current over time.

### Reading phase as an angle

Imagine two clock hands pointing at different angles. The phase difference φ is analogous to their angular separation. In the ideal current relation, the sine of that difference scales the current relative to the critical current. The hands are a way to picture a mathematical phase.

The second equation connects voltage to the rate at which phase changes. Integrating a voltage pulse over time gives its area. Pulses with different heights and widths can have the same area.

The quantization of an ideal event does not establish an error rate for a real circuit. Thermal noise, fabrication variations, drive conditions and timing differences across wires must enter circuit models and measurements.

The flux quantum Φ₀ has units of volt-seconds. Energy has units of joules. To estimate dissipation, integrate voltage times current over the event and include recovery and standby costs where relevant.

## The challenge of fabricating many small junctions

A smaller junction leaves room for more devices per unit area. Variations in critical current, interconnects, or vias can nevertheless push a large circuit outside its operating margins.

Compatibility with established manufacturing technology also leaves open which steps can be shared with a conventional CMOS transistor process. Relevant details include materials, barrier thickness, alignment between layers, inspection, and cooling conditions.

### Small devices still need room for their connections

Routing many signals requires crossings, detours and vertical connections. A via joins conductors on different layers. The multiple wiring layers and vias in imec's report address the connections necessary to go from individual junctions to circuits.[primary]

The 150 nm dimension is a junction diameter and the 30 nm dimension is a wire width. They describe different features. Likewise, three metal layers do not directly triple useful density. Insulation, alignment and the space for connections affect the achievable layout.

Manufacturing consistency requires distributions of junction properties across a chip and replication across wafers. A good micrograph provides evidence about one structure without giving process yield.

The announcement emphasizes a 300 mm platform and compatibility with existing fabrication resources. Superconducting devices still need their own material models, design rules and measurements. Available equipment and a qualified design platform are separate milestones.[primary]

### Design tools are part of the manufacturing platform

Circuit design requires more than the minimum linewidth. Designers need current characteristics, interconnect parameters, via rules and tolerances that allow an invalid combination to be detected before fabrication.

imec describes work connecting process development, electronic design automation, packaging and system integration. Future releases can show which tools, models and example circuits are actually available and validated.[primary]

## Counting refrigeration in the energy budget

The ideal upper bound on a refrigerator's coefficient of performance illustrates the cost of cooling.

<Math display tex={String.raw`\mathrm{COP}_{\rm Carnot}=\frac{T_c}{T_h-T_c}`} label="Ideal coefficient of performance of a refrigerator in absolute temperature units" />

Real refrigerators operate below this bound. Even a small heat load at the cold temperature T_c takes external work to move to room temperature T_h. Both temperatures must be in kelvin. The expression gives a theoretical limit. It does not establish the actual cooling efficiency of imec's device.

A system comparison needs the same boundary for switching losses, refrigeration load, inputs and outputs, drivers, and readout equipment. The DC resistance of a superconductor cannot by itself give a power-saving factor relative to a GPU.

### Where the heat is generated matters

Removing one watt of heat at room temperature and lifting one watt from a cryogenic stage impose different demands. In the ideal refrigeration relation above, lowering the cold-side temperature reduces the coefficient of performance. The equation explains why the refrigerator belongs in an energy comparison. It does not give the power of imec's equipment.

If signals originate at room temperature, connections span the temperature stages. Line count, data transfer, readout and control enter the system budget. The energy for one device transition differs from the energy to deliver one useful computational result.

Denser integration can place more function inside the cold volume and also complicate routing and heat management. A workload comparison therefore needs speed, errors, input/output and cooling as well as density.

## What the cross-section establishes

The published micrograph shows the metal layers, barrier, and via. It supports a measurement of wiring width, whereas the yield of a large circuit requires separate evidence. Record the design density, number of working junctions, and computational throughput separately.

As circuits grow, look for measurements of uniformity, error rate, timing margin, packaging and I/O, and energy per operation including cooling. <strong>The advance is a process for integrating superconducting devices more densely in a manufacturable structure.</strong>

### Three levels of evidence

Structural evidence includes micrographs showing layers, junctions and wiring. Electrical evidence includes current–voltage and pulse measurements under specified conditions. System evidence measures time and energy while a circuit performs a task. Each addresses a different claim.

The announcement documents process structures and development plans. A large processor's clock frequency, refrigerator efficiency, mass-production yield and measured performance against a GPU require additional evidence. Those measurements would distinguish progress at one level from progress at the next.

Superconducting computing depends on linking physical properties to fabrication, design and integration. A 30 nm wire is a concrete manufacturing result within that larger effort.

### Junction density and usable functions

Junctions per unit area indicate how many elements a design could place. Different functions require different junction counts and routing areas. Circuit layout and workload determine how much useful computation a given junction density can deliver.

Superconducting materials occur in digital and quantum circuits. The choice of material does not alone specify the encoding of information or the computational model. This announcement concerns the fabrication and scaling of digital superconducting circuits.

High-performance computing, AI and quantum control are potential application areas. Assess progress in each through the demonstrated circuits, available design environment and subsequent user validation.[primary]

### Reading electron micrographs

Read the scale bar first and identify the viewing direction. Plan views can show wire widths and layout. Cross-sections can reveal the layer stack and vertical connections. The two views answer different dimensional questions.

Then check whether the imaged sample and the device measured electrically share the same process conditions. A well-formed cross-section and a favorable electrical curve do not establish the distribution across devices. Sample counts and conditions strengthen the link between structure and function.

This distinction also applies to semiconductor materials, battery films and sensors. The presence of a structure, function in one tested condition and reproducibility in manufacturing are separate findings. imec's result extends the fabrication and interconnect evidence.
