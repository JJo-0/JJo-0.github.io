> **Joint corporate announcement: signed agreements and planned investment.** On October 6, 2026, Google and Constellation announced agreements covering power purchases and plant investment. The development was the **signing of contracts and the plan for upgrades**. It did not establish completed nuclear uprates or independently verified electricity deliveries.

## 1. What the agreement would fund

A long-term power purchase agreement (PPA) from Google helps finance Constellation’s plan to add **890 MW of generating capacity** to the PJM grid by <strong>uprating 11 existing reactors at six sites</strong>. A PPA commits a buyer to purchase future electricity over a defined period. The joint announcement anticipated the first uprate **by 2028**. Google expected the full 890 MW **before the end of 2032**. Both dates describe plans for future delivery.

## 2. Would Google alone use the electricity?

The announcement concerns the PJM grid, which connects multiple power stations. It does not describe Google owning reactors or taking exclusive delivery through a dedicated line. Existing assets would continue supplying the grid, while upgrades would add to the capacity available there. [[1]](https://www.googlecloudpresscorner.com/2026-10-06-Google-and-Constellation-Announce-Landmark-Agreement-to-Bring-890-MW-of-New-Nuclear-Capacity-to-PJM-Grid-as-Part-of-Long-Term-Power-Deal) Distinguish the buyer named in a contract from the network that receives the power.

PJM coordinates electricity supply and demand across multiple areas. To evaluate the agreement, track the capacity actually added to the grid alongside the capacity Google contracted for.

### Why invest in existing plants?

Google links the agreement to the electricity needed for growing data centers. The companies cite multiyear construction schedules and grid interconnection bottlenecks as reasons to consider existing plants. [[1]](https://www.googlecloudpresscorner.com/2026-10-06-Google-and-Constellation-Announce-Landmark-Agreement-to-Bring-890-MW-of-New-Nuclear-Capacity-to-PJM-Grid-as-Part-of-Long-Term-Power-Deal)[[2]](https://blog.google/company-news/why-were-backing-americas-existing-nuclear-plants/) Variations in wind and solar output and the discharge duration of storage also enter the comparison. [[5]](https://www.eia.gov/energyexplained/electricity/energy-storage-for-electricity-generation.php)

A long-term power purchase alone need not create **additional supply**. The relevant question is whether the purchase enables a physical increase in generating capability.

## 3. Capacity, generation, and utilization

<strong>Capacity (MW)</strong> is the maximum output a generator can provide under specified conditions. <strong>Generation (MWh)</strong> is the energy it actually produces over time. Maintaining 1 MW for one hour produces 1 MWh. Both the output level and its duration matter. [[3]](https://www.eia.gov/tools/faqs/faq.php?id=101)

<strong>Capacity factor (CF)</strong> divides actual energy generated by the energy the unit would have generated at maximum output throughout the same period. It accounts for operation at reduced output as well as downtime for maintenance. Counting only hours online gives a different measure. [[3]](https://www.eia.gov/tools/faqs/faq.php?id=101)

In this announcement, a <strong>power uprate</strong> means modifying an existing plant so that it can produce additional power. The companies describe modernization of turbines, steam generators, and digital controls. Their list does not specify an identical engineering package for each of the 11 reactors. [[1]](https://www.googlecloudpresscorner.com/2026-10-06-Google-and-Constellation-Announce-Landmark-Agreement-to-Bring-890-MW-of-New-Nuclear-Capacity-to-PJM-Grid-as-Part-of-Long-Term-Power-Deal)

## 4. What other options leave open

Options for powering AI data centers include buying existing electricity, developing new plants, contracting for renewables and storage, and restarting nuclear plants.

Selling an existing unit’s electricity to a new buyer changes who pays for it. Raising the unit’s output changes what the grid can supply. Constellation says an uprate can add capacity sooner than a new nuclear plant. This review examines the **contract linking a long-term offtake commitment to investment in existing facilities**. It did not independently verify a speed or cost advantage. [[1]](https://www.googlecloudpresscorner.com/2026-10-06-Google-and-Constellation-Announce-Landmark-Agreement-to-Bring-890-MW-of-New-Nuclear-Capacity-to-PJM-Grid-as-Part-of-Long-Term-Power-Deal)

## 5. Three elements of the arrangement

**① Long-term demand.** Google signed a **20-year PPA** for **890 MW** of additional capacity expected from Constellation’s nuclear upgrades.

**② Modifications to existing plants.** Constellation plans improvements to turbines, steam systems, controls, and other equipment at **11 reactors across six sites** in Illinois, Pennsylvania, and New Jersey. The company announced **more than $4.3 billion in investment**, including these improvements.

**③ Separate supply from existing assets.** An additional **15-year agreement covers 2,700 MW**. The companies describe it as supporting continued supply from existing generating assets. The 2,700 MW of existing supply does not add to the 890 MW of new capacity. The announcement does not establish whether all of that 2,700 MW is nuclear or specify the exact measurement basis. [[1]](https://www.googlecloudpresscorner.com/2026-10-06-Google-and-Constellation-Announce-Landmark-Agreement-to-Bring-890-MW-of-New-Nuclear-Capacity-to-PJM-Grid-as-Part-of-Long-Term-Power-Deal)

### Increasing supply and reducing demand

Separate each commitment before interpreting the numbers.

| Commitment | What it means |
|---|---|
| Purchase of 890 MW for 20 years | Supports planned uprates. The agreement does not document completed capacity or dedicated-line deliveries. |
| Supply of 2,700 MW for 15 years | Supports long-term revenue and continued output from existing assets. This is a separate category from new uprate capacity. |
| Demand adjustment during congestion | Allows reductions in noncritical consumption. The contracted MW and duration were undisclosed. Generator output is a separate quantity. |

The table separates the contract categories described in the joint announcement. It contains no measurements of power flow. [[1]](https://www.googlecloudpresscorner.com/2026-10-06-Google-and-Constellation-Announce-Landmark-Agreement-to-Bring-890-MW-of-New-Nuclear-Capacity-to-PJM-Grid-as-Part-of-Long-Term-Power-Deal)

The joint announcement also includes <strong>demand response</strong>. When the network is congested or supply is tight, consumers can change how much electricity they use or when they use it. The agreement includes a capability to curtail noncritical consumption during stressed grid conditions. [[1]](https://www.googlecloudpresscorner.com/2026-10-06-Google-and-Constellation-Announce-Landmark-Agreement-to-Bring-890-MW-of-New-Nuclear-Capacity-to-PJM-Grid-as-Part-of-Long-Term-Power-Deal)

An uprate aims to increase available generating output. Demand response reduces the power needed at particular times. Both affect the supply–demand balance, but load that can be curtailed cannot also be counted as new generator capacity. The announcement gave no guaranteed demand-response capacity, duration, or record of actual dispatches.

The companies also agreed on a **strategic framework for exploring** additional clean generation, storage, and demand response. It does not establish that a particular additional plant or storage project has been completed. Distinguish the 890 MW uprate plan, the separate existing-asset supply agreement, future opportunities, and the demand-response capability. [[1]](https://www.googlecloudpresscorner.com/2026-10-06-Google-and-Constellation-Announce-Landmark-Agreement-to-Bring-890-MW-of-New-Nuclear-Capacity-to-PJM-Grid-as-Part-of-Long-Term-Power-Deal)

**Check your understanding.** If a data center cuts its consumption for one hour, has the reactor’s maximum output increased by the same amount? The reduction changes demand in that hour. Comparing its contribution with an uprate requires supply and consumption measurements over matching periods and the performance conditions of each commitment.

## 6. How much energy could 890 MW produce in a year?

Start with a smaller case. A constant 1 MW for ten hours produces 10 MWh. If average output is half the maximum over those hours, it produces 5 MWh. This illustration uses the EIA definitions of capacity, generation, and capacity factor, rather than measurements from this deal. [[3]](https://www.eia.gov/tools/faqs/faq.php?id=101)

Estimate energy from the added capacity by multiplying capacity, duration, and the capacity factor over that period. This is an educational unit conversion, independent of the corporate announcement.

| Symbol | Meaning, unit, and assumption |
|---|---|
| E | Generation attributable to the added capacity over the period, in MWh. |
| ΔP | Additional capacity on a consistent output basis, in MW. Assumed constant throughout the period. |
| T | Hours in the period. A 365-day year has 365 × 24 = 8,760 hours. |
| CF | Actual generation divided by generation at continuous maximum output. Dimensionless. The example assumes 0.90. |

**Worked example.** Suppose the full 890 MW is available for an entire 365-day year at a capacity factor of 0.90. Then 890 × 8,760 × 0.90 = 7,016,760 MWh, or about 7.02 TWh. Multiplying MW by hours gives MWh. CF does not change the unit, and one TWh is one million MWh.

The 890 MW is the announced target. The 0.90 factor is an author-selected assumption. The actual capacity factor of the increment, the net or gross basis, and annual deliveries were not confirmed. This example does not measure the uprate increment by applying a plant-wide historical average to it.

**Check your understanding.** Would a phased rollout during the year give the same energy in year one? **No.** The calculation assumes all capacity is available for 8,760 hours. A first-year estimate should use the capacity and hours available at each unit, with consistent definitions of output and generation. Treating the rollout as a single full-year block could overstate generation.

## 8. Which outcomes remain unverified?

Signing the agreement does not measure additional generation. **The 890 MW is a future capacity target.** It does not establish that unit-specific changes to operating licenses or validation of plant performance are complete. This distinction records the evidence available at the announcement date. It is not evidence that the plan has failed.

The reviewed joint release and Google explanation do not give MW by plant, a detailed capital-expenditure breakdown, the PPA price, actual utilization, or final grid acceptance conditions. The documents describe an agreement targeting additional capacity. Actual delivery requires separate evidence.

## 9. Limitations

The corporate announcements alone cannot establish unit-specific licensing or safety assessments, detailed modifications, or grid conditions. The NRC’s backgrounder explains that increasing a plant’s licensed maximum power requires NRC approval and review of analyses submitted with the operating-license amendment application. [[4]](https://www.nrc.gov/regulations-legislation/fact-sheets-brochures/backgrounder-on-power-uprates-for-nuclear-plants) The generic process does not show the application or approval status for these 11 units. This review did not verify specific engineering constraints or regulatory approvals for them.

A large customer’s long-term contract also does not automatically lower retail rates for all other consumers. An economic assessment requires contract pricing and allocation of grid costs.

## 10. What could change?

Google becomes a contracted buyer whose long-term commitment supports Constellation’s investment in its existing reactors. That is the role described by the companies. It does not establish an industry-wide change in how AI companies procure power.

If the uprates succeed, the region gains generating capacity beyond a simple rearrangement of existing purchases. Alongside the PPA price, track **when actual incremental MW become available on the grid**.

## 11. Developments to follow

Watch for NRC applications and approvals, allocation of MW across the 11 units, completion of turbine and control upgrades, **metered evidence of the first delivery expected in 2028**, PJM capacity recognition, disclosures on pricing and cost allocation, and measured operating indicators for the use of Gemini.
