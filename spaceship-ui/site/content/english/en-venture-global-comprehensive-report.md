# Venture Global (VG) investment report

> Report date — 2026-03-24
> Purpose — To understand Venture Global’s results in the context of the **entire liquefied natural gas (LNG) industry**.
> Companion reading — [VG’s 2026 revenue forecast memo](#venture-global-vg-2026-revenue-forecast-memo-revised-methodology).

---

## 1. Investment thesis at a glance

- Venture Global plays a **central role in expanding U.S. LNG supply**.
- Its investment case rests on successive capacity additions at **Calcasieu Pass, Plaquemines, and CP2**. LNG prices are only one part of that case.
- Near-term variables include the Plaquemines ramp-up, changes in 2026 revenue and EBITDA, and risks tied to policy, shipping, and weather.
- As U.S. and Qatari capacity enters the market, the medium-term question is **how quickly VG can bring its new capacity into commercial operation**.
- Over the longer term, contract terms, cost competitiveness, methane and policy requirements, and access to financing will shape its valuation.

## 1.1 March 24, 2026 update

- This edition includes developments reported during March 2026.
- Five developments matter for the analysis.
  1. Agreements with Hanwha on `2026-02-27`, Trafigura on `2026-03-02`, and Vitol on `2026-03-23` raised the long-term contract portfolio to `>46 MTPA` (million tonnes per annum).
  2. The FY2025 and fourth-quarter release on `2026-03-02`, the Department of Energy (DOE) approval for Plaquemines on `2026-03-13`, and the Federal Register notice on `2026-03-27` clarified project and regulatory milestones.
  3. The final investment decision (FID) and financial close for CP2 Phase 2 on `2026-03-13` made the medium-term growth plan more concrete.
  4. The Edison settlement on `2026-03-26` reduced arbitration uncertainty. The incremental volume remained undisclosed.
  5. Reuters reports on the war and logistics on `2026-03-19/25/26` informed the market assessment. Their implications for quantitative inputs remained provisional.

## 2. Company overview

## 2.1 What the company does

Venture Global develops, produces, and exports LNG from the U.S. Gulf Coast. Three assets anchor the portfolio.

| Asset | Status | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| **Calcasieu Pass** | Operating commercially | First cash-generating asset |
| **Plaquemines LNG** | Commissioning and ramping up toward its commercial operations date (COD) | Principal near-term driver of earnings and share price |
| **CP2 LNG** | Next major expansion project | Main source of potential medium-term valuation gains |

## 2.2 Why the market follows VG

- It sits at the center of U.S. LNG capacity expansion.
- Its installed capacity is growing rapidly.
- Its portfolio combines long-term sales and purchase agreements (SPAs) with uncontracted or flexible volumes.
- Successful execution at Plaquemines and CP2 could prompt the market to value VG as a **large, multi-project LNG operator**.

## 2.3 Company milestones to track

- **Calcasieu Pass** entered full commercial operation on 2025-04-15.
- **Plaquemines** is a major growth asset with roughly **27.2 MTPA of nameplate capacity**. DOE approval on 2026-03-13 expanded its export options.
- **CP2** is the next core asset. Its investment decision and financial close were confirmed in March 2026.
- **Contracts.** The Hanwha SPA on 2026-02-27 and the Trafigura and Vitol agreements on 2026-03-02 and 2026-03-23 lifted the long-term book to `>46 MTPA`.
- **Long-term objective.** VG intends to build a large U.S. LNG business across multiple projects.

### Europe

- European gas demand could trend lower over the long term. Storage and winter temperatures can still cause substantial swings in near-term LNG imports.
- For VG, Europe can **absorb additional cargoes when supplies are tight**. That role is distinct from sustained growth in demand.

### Asia

- Asia is the more important source of long-term demand growth.
- In price-sensitive markets, high spot prices can reduce consumption. More stable prices may attract additional demand.
- Shipping U.S. LNG to Asia costs more than shipping it to Europe. A sufficiently wide price spread can also offer VG greater upside on Asian deliveries.

### What the two markets offer VG

- European demand provides a **defensive outlet**.
- Asian demand offers **long-term growth and potential gains from wider spreads**.
- The same cargo can command different values in the two markets. **Destination economics** therefore matter alongside volume.

## 4.1 Which prices affect VG?

Three benchmarks matter for a U.S. LNG exporter.

1. **Henry Hub** measures the U.S. feed gas price.
2. **Europe / TTF** is the benchmark for the European destination market.
3. **JKM (Japan Korea Marker), or a public proxy,** represents the Asian destination market.

## 4.2 The spread that determines export economics

A high international LNG price is only one element in VG’s calculation.

```text
International LNG price - U.S. gas price - liquefaction, shipping, and other costs
```

The amount left after those costs is the relevant spread.

## 4.3 Implications for VG

- Low Henry Hub prices and high international prices are favorable.
- Rising U.S. gas prices or freight costs put pressure on margins.
- A widening Europe–Asia price difference can change where a cargo earns the highest return.

### Prices in March 2026

- After war broke out in early March, JKM and TTF rose sharply while Henry Hub moved less.
- This combination could **improve U.S. LNG export economics**.
- For an exporter with flexible volumes, such as VG, **wider spreads and the ability to redirect cargoes** can matter more than the rise in the headline price.

- A benchmark for its ability to deliver projects.

## 5.2 Plaquemines LNG

- The central project in the current investment case.
- Track `commissioning`, `ramp-up`, `Phase I COD`, and `Phase II COD`.
- Plaquemines supplied a large share of 2025 volumes and remains the main 2026 growth driver.

## 6.1 Why contract terms matter

Building a liquefaction plant is only part of an LNG project. The economics depend on **who will buy the output, in what volumes, for how many years, and at what price**.

## 6.2 Contract terms to track

- Share covered by long-term SPAs
- Share of cargoes already contracted
- Exposure to uncontracted cargoes
- fixed liquefaction fee vs variable charges
- Mix of FOB and DES delivery terms

## 6.3 VG’s current position

- Contracts cover **69% of 2026 cargoes**, cushioning downside under VG’s investor relations and SEC disclosures of 2026-03-02.
- The remaining cargoes retain exposure to both stronger and weaker markets.
- VG combines features of a **tolling business and a spot merchant**.

### Recent developments in the contract portfolio

- Alongside long-term SPAs, VG has signed **medium-term agreements** to balance cash-flow stability with the ability to respond to market changes.
- The mix preserves more upside than a fully fixed arrangement and offers more downside protection than a wholly spot-exposed business.
- That flexibility may be especially valuable when international markets change abruptly, as they did after the outbreak of war.

## 6.4 Investment interpretation

- Greater contract coverage makes cash flow easier to forecast.
- Flexible volumes allow VG to capture additional returns in strong markets.
- VG’s appeal comes from that **combination of stability and flexibility**.

## 6.5 Arbitration / legal overhang

- Disputes can lead investors to apply a valuation discount.
- The Edison settlement disclosed on SEC Form 8-K on 2026-03-26 reduced part of this uncertainty. Completion was targeted for the end of Q2 2026.
- Additional European cargo deliveries and a first May 2026 delivery to Adriatic LNG were confirmed. The incremental volume remained undisclosed.
- Disputes affect **perceptions of governance, confidence in contracts, and valuation multiples** as well as direct legal costs.

### How to interpret developments

- Lower arbitration risk could reduce the discount applied for execution uncertainty.
- A renewed dispute could hurt valuation more than earnings.

## 7.1 U.S. policy

As a U.S. exporter, VG is directly exposed to decisions by the DOE and the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC).

- The DOE authorizes exports.
- FERC approves terminal siting, construction, and operation.
- EPA regulation and environmental review under NEPA can affect permit timing.

## 7.2 International geopolitics

- A tight European gas balance
- Risk in the Middle East
- Changes in the relative supply roles of Russia, Qatar, and the United States
- Bottlenecks at canals and straits

### 7.2A March 2026 geopolitical update

- The DOE approval on 2026-03-13 and Federal Register notice on 2026-03-27 both sent favorable policy signals, but they represent different stages. One authorized additional exports and the other concerned an application.
- The disruption to Qatari supply emerged as a major variable for the 2026 LNG market.
- Some reports raised the possibility of structural damage to part of the capacity, with potential effects extending beyond **weeks or months** of disruption.
- The report treats statements by Trump or other policymakers as confirmed developments only when they lead to signed DOE or FERC action, or to a contract approval.

## 7.3 Effects on VG

- Easier policy could raise the value of projects in the pipeline, including CP2.
- Tighter policy could slow permitting and expansion.
- International supply disruptions could **already be creating near-term upside** in spot sales and margins.

### Policy issues to watch

- U.S. LNG policy remains sensitive to the position of the administration in office.
- VG’s shares therefore have some **sensitivity to policy shifts** as well as company execution.
- A favorable policy direction could reduce the valuation discount on assets such as CP2.
- The additional Plaquemines export approval illustrates how favorable policy can give VG near-term commercial options more quickly.

## 8.1 Why shipping is central to LNG economics

One reason LNG is less fungible than oil is the complexity of its supply chain.

- Liquefaction facilities
- Dedicated LNG carriers
- Canals and straits
- Regasification terminals

## 8.2 Shipping considerations for VG

- Differences between Gulf Coast routes to Europe and Asia
- Panama Canal, Suez Canal, and alternative routes
- Exposure to long-term charter rates and spot freight
- Direct exposure to shipping costs under DES contracts

### What this means for VG

- VG is increasingly viewed as an operator with **control over logistics** as well as terminals.
- Owning and expanding a carrier fleet can improve its response to disruption and its ability to redirect cargoes.
- A larger fleet also increases capital needs and operational complexity.

### 8.2A March 2026 logistics update

- The market saw **cargo diversions** as more Atlantic cargoes were redirected to Asia.
- For U.S. exporters such as VG, those diversions make **destination economics increasingly important** alongside changes in freight rates.
- The shipping decision therefore includes **where to send the cargo** as well as the cost of carrying it.
- Changes in freight, insurance, or basis become material to the forecast memo only when they can be linked to disclosed margins or the destination mix.

## 8.3 Investment interpretation

- A freight spike can put pressure on EBITDA.
- Bottlenecks can affect cargo timing and when revenue is recognized.
- Assessing VG therefore requires following the **logistics chain** alongside LNG prices.

# Venture Global (VG) 2026 revenue forecast memo (revised methodology)

> Report date — 2026-03-24 (rev. 2)
> Source material — Venture Global’s 4Q/FY2025 earnings release (2026-03-02), SEC Form 8-K and Exhibit 99.1, and the 4Q2025 investor presentation.
> Purpose — Estimate 2026 **GAAP revenue** using inputs that distinguish revenue from EBITDA and liquefaction fees.

---

## 1) What changed in this revision

- The previous version generally quoted the official figures correctly and identified the overall direction of change.
- Three methodological issues required correction.
  1. It used the `fixed liquefaction fee` as if it were the `GAAP revenue per unit`.
  2. It mixed `exported cargoes` with `sold TBtu` when calculating volume per cargo.
  3. It translated a decline in EBITDA directly into a sharp decline in unit revenue.

The revised method addresses these issues using four principles.

1. **Estimate revenue from revenue inputs.**
2. Use **fees and EBITDA as cross-checks**.
3. **Separate exported volume from sold volume** to keep the units consistent.
4. Incorporate March 2026 contract, regulatory, and legal developments in the investment thesis. **Model change: No.** Revenue calculations stay in place unless the developments change `2026 cargo guidance, the sold/export ratio, or GAAP revenue per MMBtu`.

## 3) What each measure estimates

### 3.1 GAAP Revenue

- The `Revenue` line on the income statement.
- The reported amount reflects contract structure, delivery terms, variable charges, and the timing of recognition.

### 3.2 Fixed liquefaction fee (unsold cargos)

- One price input for the remaining unsold cargoes.
- The company provided the figure to illustrate EBITDA sensitivity.
- **The average unit price for GAAP revenue measures something different.**

### 3.3 Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA

- A non-GAAP performance measure reflecting costs and adjustments relative to revenue.
- There is no single formula that converts it back into revenue on a one-to-one basis.

Use the `fee` and `EBITDA` as **cross-checks** for the revenue estimate. Estimating revenue itself requires separate inputs.

---

## 4) Revised framework for estimating 2026 revenue

## 4.1 Step A — Estimate export volume

First, calculate exported TBtu per cargo from 2025 actuals.

```text
1,415.4 / 380 = 3.725 TBtu/cargo
```

Apply the 2026 guidance for exported cargoes.

```text
Low export TBtu  = 486 × 3.725 = 1,810.35 TBtu
High export TBtu = 527 × 3.725 = 1,963.08 TBtu
```

## 4.2 Step B — Convert to a consistent sold-volume basis

The 2025 ratio of sold to exported volume was as follows.

```text
sold ratio = 1,408.8 / 1,415.4 = 0.99534
```

Use that ratio to estimate 2026 sold TBtu.

```text
Low sold TBtu  = 1,810.35 × 0.99534 = 1,801.9
High sold TBtu = 1,963.08 × 0.99534 = 1,953.9
Mid sold TBtu  = (Low + High) / 2 = 1,877.9
```

This sequence connects `exported cargoes` with `sold TBtu` while keeping each quantity on its own basis.

## 4.3 Step C — Estimate realized GAAP revenue per unit

Start with actual 2025 realized GAAP revenue per unit.

```text
13,769 / 1,408.8 = $9.77/MMBtu
```

The 2026 scenarios allow for **a weaker, middle, and stronger realization** after accounting for the following factors.

- Adjusted EBITDA guidance below the 2025 result
- First-quarter margin compression and pressure from basis and shipping
- Higher volume and the possibility of retaining some variable charges

Assumed unit revenue by scenario

- Bear: **$8.2/MMBtu**
- Base: **$8.8/MMBtu**
- Bull: **$9.4/MMBtu**

These are **blended realized unit-revenue assumptions for GAAP revenue**. The `fixed liquefaction fee` covers only one part of the economics.

## 4.4 Quantifying the change from 2025 actuals to 2026 scenario prices

The table starts from 2025 actual unit revenue, then adds or subtracts assumptions for 2026 conditions. All adjustments are in dollars per MMBtu.

```text
2025 actual realized revenue = $9.77/MMBtu
```

| Component | Bear | Base | Bull | Explanation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 2025 actual anchor | 9.77 | 9.77 | 9.77 | Realized 2025 GAAP revenue per unit |
| Margin compression, basis, and shipping pressure | -1.05 | -0.75 | -0.45 | Downward pressure on profitability identified in company commentary |
| Normalization of variable charges and recognition timing | -0.35 | -0.20 | -0.05 | Adjustment as commissioning and timing effects normalize |
| Operational stability and mix as volume increases | -0.17 | -0.02 | +0.18 | Partial recovery possible as ramp-up progresses |
| **2026 assumed price** | **8.20** | **8.80** | **9.40** | Input to the revenue calculation |

How the scenarios differ
- The bear case assumes persistent, strong pressure from margins, shipping, and basis.
- The base case assumes some normalization while downward pressure continues.
- The bull case assumes that pressures ease and the sales mix improves more substantially.

The following formula gives revenue in millions of dollars.

```text
Revenue = sold TBtu × realized GAAP revenue ($/MMBtu)
```


→ **$14.8bn**


## 7) Cross-check against EBITDA and fee guidance

## 7.1 Implied EBITDA margins

Using $5.5bn as the midpoint of 2026 EBITDA guidance gives the following margins.

- Bear margin = 5.5 / 14.8 = **37.2%**
- Base margin = 5.5 / 16.5 = **33.3%**
- Bull margin = 5.5 / 18.4 = **29.9%**

All three are below the 2025 EBITDA margin of 45.5%, consistent with the direction of the margin compression described by the company.

## 7.2 How to interpret fee sensitivity

- Read the `$5–6 fixed fee` and the sensitivity of `±$575–625m in EBITDA per ±$1` together.
  - They describe **EBITDA sensitivity on remaining unsold cargoes**.
  - Calculating the overall revenue price requires inputs for other cargoes and charges.

The revised revenue formula therefore has no direct input for the fee metric.

## 8) Why the base estimate rose from $14.1bn

The earlier memo used fee economics to estimate `GAAP revenue per unit`, pushing its central estimate too low.

This revision makes three changes.

1. Set the unit-revenue starting point on a <strong>GAAP basis using 2025 actuals</strong>.
2. Put volumes on a consistent basis by **converting exports to sales**.
3. Use EBITDA and fees only for **cross-checks**.

Together these revisions raise the central estimate from $14.1bn to $16.5bn.

## 9) Risks and rules for updating the model

Recalculate the estimate with each quarterly update.

1. Update the `sold/export ratio` using cumulative data through the latest quarter.
2. Update `realized GAAP revenue per MMBtu` on the same cumulative basis.
3. Reflect any revised guidance for `exported cargoes` over the remainder of 2026.
4. Lower the unit-revenue assumptions if company commentary indicates worsening basis or shipping pressure.
5. Classify contract or offtake announcements, DOE or FERC actions, arbitration developments, and policy headlines as **qualitative-only** unless they change cargo guidance, the sold/export ratio, or the GAAP unit-revenue input. Otherwise record **Model change: No**.

## 9.1 Quarterly recalculation template

Replace the inputs at the next quarterly release to recalculate the estimate.

### Inputs

| Variable | Symbol | Current example |
|---|---|---:|
| 2025 exported cargos | `C_2025` | 380 |
| 2025 exported TBtu | `E_2025` | 1,415.4 |
| 2025 sold TBtu | `S_2025` | 1,408.8 |
| 2026 cargo low/high | `C_low`, `C_high` | 486, 527 |
| 2026 unit-revenue assumptions | `P_bear`, `P_base`, `P_bull` | 8.2, 8.8, 9.4 |

### Derived values

```text
TBtu_per_cargo = E_2025 / C_2025
sold_ratio = S_2025 / E_2025

Sold_low  = C_low  × TBtu_per_cargo × sold_ratio
Sold_mid  = ((C_low + C_high) / 2) × TBtu_per_cargo × sold_ratio
Sold_high = C_high × TBtu_per_cargo × sold_ratio
```

### Revenue outputs

```text
Rev_bear = Sold_low  × P_bear
Rev_base = Sold_mid  × P_base
Rev_bull = Sold_high × P_bull
```


## 11) Glossary for readers new to LNG

These terms recur in LNG financial reporting. Each entry explains how the term is used in this forecast.

### Anchor

- **Definition.** The starting value for a calculation.
- **In this memo.** Actual realized GAAP revenue of `$9.77/MMBtu` in 2025.
- **Why it matters.** A result the company has already achieved gives the forecast a measurable starting point.

### GAAP Revenue

- **Definition.** Revenue recognized in the income statement under generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP).
- **What it reflects.** Contract structure, delivery terms, variable charges, and timing.
- **Why it matters.** This reported `Revenue` is what the model estimates.

### Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA

- **Definition.** A company-reported `non-GAAP` measure of profitability, distinct from GAAP revenue and net income.
- **In practice.** It gives investors one way to assess earnings from operations.
- **Why it matters.** Revenue and EBITDA can move together, but EBITDA alone cannot determine revenue.

### Non-GAAP

- **Definition.** A supplementary measure a company reports outside the metrics defined by GAAP.
- **Example.** Adjusted EBITDA.
- **Why it matters.** These metrics can help assess underlying business performance. They use a different measurement basis from GAAP revenue.

### Margin compression

- **Definition.** A decline in the percentage of revenue retained as earnings.
- **In practice.** The company keeps less of each dollar of sales.
- **Why it matters.** VG guided to lower 2026 EBITDA despite higher expected volumes, suggesting profitability could weaken even as it sells more.

### Basis impact

- **Definition.** The effect on earnings of the difference between a benchmark price and the actual purchase or transaction price.
- **In practice.** Where and on what terms a company sources gas affect the margin it earns.
- **Why it matters.** Basis can affect EBITDA more directly than revenue. VG cited it as a headwind when explaining 2026 guidance.

### Shipping cost

- **Definition.** The cost of transporting LNG.
- **In practice.** This includes vessel costs, freight rates, and associated logistics expenses.
- **Why it matters.** Increased freight costs can reduce EBITDA even if revenue stays the same.

### MMBtu

- **Definition.** A unit of energy.
- **In full.** Million British thermal units.
- **In practice.** LNG prices are commonly quoted per MMBtu.
- **Why it matters.** `$9.77/MMBtu` expresses the amount realized per unit of energy sold.

### TBtu

- **Definition.** Trillion British thermal units.
- **Conversion.** `1 TBtu = 1,000,000 MMBtu`.
- **Why it matters.** Companies often report total sales and exports in TBtu and unit revenue in dollars per MMBtu. The conversion connects them.

### Exported cargos

- **Definition.** The number of LNG cargoes physically loaded and exported.
- **In practice.** Each shipment counts once.
- **Why it matters.** VG’s 2026 guidance is stated in cargoes, making this the starting point for the volume estimate.

### Sold volumes and sold TBtu

- **Definition.** The volume recorded as sales for accounting purposes.
- **In practice.** A shipment counts here when the company recognizes the revenue.
- **Why it matters.** Calculating revenue requires a `sold` volume measure alongside the `exported` volume measure.

### Sold / Export ratio

- **Definition.** The ratio of volume recognized as sold to volume exported.
- **In this memo.** `1,408.8 / 1,415.4 = 0.99534`.
- **Why it matters.** It translates export cargo guidance into the sold-volume input used to estimate revenue.

### Fixed liquefaction fee

- **Definition.** A fixed charge collected for liquefaction services.
- **In practice.** It is the basic fee for converting gas into LNG.
- **Why it matters.** It informs EBITDA sensitivity. Average GAAP revenue per unit across the company includes additional components.

### Variable charge

- **Definition.** A variable fee or settlement component charged in addition to the fixed fee.
- **In practice.** It adds an amount that changes with the relevant conditions.
- **Why it matters.** The statement `fixed fee excludes variable charges` means that other charges must also be considered when estimating revenue.

### Contract structure

- **Definition.** The terms under which LNG is sold to a particular buyer.
- **What it covers.** Duration, price indexation, delivery terms, and volume obligations.
- **Why it matters.** Contract terms can produce significant differences in revenue and margins on otherwise similar LNG.

### SPA (Sales and Purchase Agreement)

- **Definition.** A long-term LNG sale and purchase agreement.
- **In practice.** The buyer and seller agree on volumes, a pricing structure, and a term of years.
- **Why it matters.** More contracted volumes give a project greater certainty over sales and can make an FID more likely.

### Contracted cargos

- **Definition.** Cargoes for which contracts are already in place.
- **Why it matters.** With 69% of 2026 cargoes contracted, overall upside and downside reflect the contracted volumes as well as the uncontracted portion.

### Delivery terms

- **Definition.** The conditions assigning responsibility for delivery and specifying where it passes to the buyer.
- **Why it matters.** Those terms affect freight costs, revenue recognition timing, and the allocation of margins.

### FOB (Free on Board)

- **Definition.** The seller delivers at the loading port. The buyer is responsible for subsequent transportation.
- **In practice.** The seller’s delivery responsibility ends when the LNG is loaded onto the ship.
- **Why it matters.** With less freight exposure for the seller, reported revenue and margins can differ from a DES sale.

### DES (Delivered Ex-Ship)

- **Definition.** The seller transports the LNG to the destination for delivery.
- **In practice.** The seller is responsible for the voyage to the agreed destination.
- **Why it matters.** Freight and timing have a greater effect, making revenue and margins more complex to interpret.

### Revenue recognition timing

- **Definition.** The point at which a company records revenue for accounting purposes.
- **In practice.** An LNG transaction may be recognized in a different quarter from the shipment.
- **Why it matters.** Timing is one reason exported and sold volumes may differ.

### Commissioning cargo

- **Definition.** A cargo produced during testing and commissioning around the transition to commercial operation.
- **In practice.** A plant can produce early cargoes before reaching normal operation.
- **Why it matters.** These volumes have different economics from normalized long-term contracted volumes and can temporarily distort reported revenue and margins.

### Ramp-up

- **Definition.** The increase in output from initial startup to normal production levels.
- **In practice.** The plant gradually approaches full operation.
- **Why it matters.** The pace of the Plaquemines ramp-up is a major driver of VG’s 2026 results.

### Midpoint

- **Definition.** The value halfway between the high and low ends of guidance.
- **Example.** EBITDA guidance of $5.2bn to $5.8bn has a midpoint of $5.5bn.
- **Why it matters.** The midpoint is a common starting point for a base case.
