## 2.3 Underfitting and overfitting


{/* source-content:P2-C043 */}
The central machine-learning challenge is maintaining performance on inputs not used in training. This is generalization.


{/* source-content:P2-C044 */}
Optimizing parameters to reduce training-set error is an optimization problem. Machine learning adds the requirement of low generalization or test error: expected error on new inputs.


{/* source-content:P2-C045 */}
The linear-regression example evaluates the model from equation (1) on a test set.


{/* source-content:P2-C046 */}
<SourceFormula formula={part2Formula('MAI-P2-034')} display={true} />


{/* source-content:P2-C047 */}
Here, M is the number of test samples.


{/* source-content:P2-C048 */}
Training and test data are modeled as draws from a data-generating distribution. The typical i.i.d. assumption makes samples independent and gives training and test sets the same distribution.


{/* source-content:P2-C049 */}
For a procedure that samples training data, optimizes parameters, and then samples test data, the lecture explains that expected test error is no smaller than expected training error.


{/* source-content:P2-C050 */}
<SourceFormula formula={part2Formula('MAI-P2-035')} display={true} />


{/* source-content:P2-C051 */}
- A good learning algorithm should achieve low training error.


{/* source-content:P2-C052 */}
- It should also keep the gap between training and test errors small.


{/* source-content:P2-C053 */}
<strong>Underfitting</strong> occurs when the model cannot obtain sufficiently low error even on training data.


{/* source-content:P2-C054 */}
<strong>Overfitting</strong> occurs when the training–test error gap is excessively large.


{/* source-content:P2-C055 */}
Changing model capacity controls these possibilities. Low capacity may fail to represent the training set; high capacity may memorize properties that do not help on test data.

## 2.4 Hyperparameters and validation sets


{/* source-content:P2-C073 */}
Most learning algorithms have hyperparameters controlling their behavior. These are not the model parameters adapted by the algorithm itself.


{/* source-content:P2-C074 */}
Polynomial degree in Figure 1 is a capacity hyperparameter, as is weight-decay λ.


{/* source-content:P2-C075 */}
Some settings remain hyperparameters because optimization is difficult. More commonly, learning them on the training set is inappropriate: choosing capacity there can favor the maximum available capacity and overfit.


{/* source-content:P2-C076 */}
A validation set unseen by the training algorithm addresses this problem.


{/* source-content:P2-C077 */}
### 2.4.1 Training, validation, and test sets


{/* source-content:P2-C078 */}
- **Training set:** learns model parameters.


{/* source-content:P2-C079 */}
- **Validation set:** estimates generalization to unseen data and selects hyperparameters and configurations.


{/* source-content:P2-C080 */}
- **Test set:** evaluates the final frozen model on genuinely unseen data for an unbiased generalization estimate.

### 2.4.2 Cross-validation


{/* source-content:P2-C082 */}
With a small test set in a fixed split, statistical uncertainty in mean test error makes claims that algorithm A beats B difficult.


{/* source-content:P2-C083 */}
This matters less with hundreds of thousands of samples. Small datasets can instead spend extra computation on repeated training and evaluation across splits, using all samples to estimate mean test error.


{/* source-content:P2-C084 */}
The usual K-fold procedure divides data into K disjoint subsets and runs K experiments. Run k tests on subset k and trains on the remainder; average the K test errors.

# Research update: August 18, 2026

> The following is not PDF source material. It tracks extensions of the lecture's questions through peer-reviewed 2025–2026 papers and official proceedings.


## 1. Generalization extends beyond new samples from the same distribution

The i.i.d. framework remains a theoretical reference, but deployment changes sensors, regions, user groups, time, class ratios, and background correlations. Distinguish **in-distribution performance**, **distribution shift**, **OOD generalization**, and **OOD detection**.

- **ICML 2025, peer-reviewed:** Qiang et al. analyze how self-supervised learning can acquire spurious correlations and lose OOD generalization, proposing batch construction targeting a causal post-intervention distribution. [PMLR paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/qiang25a.html)
- **ICML 2025, peer-reviewed:** Harun et al. report a layerwise trade-off in which stronger neural collapse can help OOD detection yet hurt OOD generalization. Rejecting unfamiliar inputs and predicting well on them are different objectives. [PMLR paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/harun25a.html)
- **UAI 2025, peer-reviewed:** Cai et al. distinguish ordinary out-of-sample robustness from OOD robustness with different training and test environments, showing that structural assumptions such as covariate or label shift may be necessary for nontrivial solutions. [PMLR paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v286/cai25c.html)

Modern test sets should therefore include **environment, temporal, group, and difficulty splits** specifying anticipated changes, rather than one random holdout alone.

## 2. Overparameterized models challenge “zero training error means overfitting”

Classical capacity diagrams suggest worsening test error after fitting training data exactly. Modern research studies **double descent**, where risk falls again beyond interpolation; **benign overfitting**, which fits noise yet generalizes; and **implicit regularization**, through which optimization selects particular solutions.

- **ICML 2025, peer-reviewed:** Xu and Chen analyze two-layer networks with class-dependent heterogeneous noise, showing how some long-tail noise can aid implicit feature learning. Benign versus harmful overfitting depends on data structure and learning dynamics, not parameter count alone. [PMLR paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/xu25d.html)
- **ICML 2025, peer-reviewed:** Sakamoto and Sato analyze signal-to-noise conditions under which attention token selection generalizes despite fitting label noise. [PMLR paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/sakamoto25a.html)
- **COLT 2026, peer-reviewed extended abstract:** Wu et al. compare finite-sample risks of early-stopped GD, ridge, and online SGD instance by instance in linear regression. GD can dominate ridge within constants in some regimes, yet SGD can be polynomially better than optimally stopped GD in benign-overfitting-type problems. No regularizer is always superior. [PMLR paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v336/wu26a.html)

The capacity diagram remains introductory, but examine **function spaces, data spectra, margins, optimization paths, and stopping times** too.

## 3. Repeatedly reused validation data become training information

Define train/validation/test by **information flow**, not labels. Repeatedly consulting the same evaluation results during development fits the research process to that set, even without direct model gradients.

- **NeurIPS 2025 Datasets & Benchmarks, peer-reviewed:** TabArena proposes a living benchmark addressing static-benchmark defects and aging versions. Time budgets, ensembles, and validation choices substantially affect rankings; overrepresentation of some models in cross-model ensembles is linked to validation overfitting. [Official proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/1697e3fb412da11dc9488249f9e7bbc9-Abstract-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.html)
- **NeurIPS 2025 Datasets & Benchmarks, peer-reviewed:** The Scientific ML Common Task Framework proposes standardized tasks, realistic noise, limited data, and genuinely hidden tests to reduce weak baselines, reporting bias, and inconsistent evaluation. [Official proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/f908bc294862a555413fbe43ff08933f-Abstract-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.html)
- **COLT 2026, peer-reviewed:** Nachum et al. analyze k-fold CV as a data-reusing risk estimator and give unavoidable minimax MSE lower bounds when fold count grows with sample size. Cross-validation is not an infinitely precise evaluator. [PMLR paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v336/nachum26a.html)

Practical protocols should include **group/patient/entity splits, temporal splits, fitting preprocessing and feature selection within folds, nested CV, one final test use, and disclosed seeds/folds**.

## 4. Accuracy alone does not describe risk: calibration and conformal prediction

Calibration asks whether predictions assigned probability 0.9 are correct about 90% of the time. Good aggregate calibration can still fail for subgroups, OOD inputs, or temporal changes. Conformal prediction provides finite-sample coverage under exchangeability or i.i.d. assumptions, which must be revisited under shift.

- **ICML 2025, peer-reviewed:** Van der Laan and Alaa extend Venn/Venn–Abers methods to general losses, proposing finite-sample set-valued calibration and subpopulation multicalibration. [PMLR paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/van-der-laan25a.html)
- **ICML 2025, peer-reviewed:** Bashari, Sesia, and Romano analyze partly contaminated reference sets for conformal outlier detection. Under nonadversarial conditions, type-I error remains conservatively controlled while power declines. [PMLR paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/bashari25a.html)
- **ALT 2026, peer-reviewed:** Vovk quantifies efficiency universality of conformal predictors under i.i.d. randomness. This supports their strength, not unconditional validity under shift. [PMLR paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v313/vovk26a.html)

Alongside accuracy/F1, record **reliability diagrams, ECE bin dependence, NLL/Brier scores, prediction-set coverage and size, subgroup coverage, and shift-specific performance**.

## 5. From observed dimension to effective dimension and inductive bias

Handling high-dimensional inputs does not eliminate dimensionality limits. Ask which low-dimensional structures models actually exploit and whether those structures persist in testing.

- **ICML 2025, peer-reviewed:** Dong et al. analyze weak-to-strong fine-tuning through ridgeless regression, explaining variance reduction through adaptation in a low-intrinsic-dimensional subspace. [PMLR paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/dong25g.html)
- **ICML 2025, peer-reviewed:** Chen et al. show that low-to-high-dimensional generalization requires suitable inductive bias; architecture and (S)GD must select interpolators aligned with the target. [PMLR paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/chen25cb.html)
- **COLT 2026, peer-reviewed open problem:** Feldman, Kamath, and Srebro formally ask whether deep learning's advantage over linear models is inherently distribution-dependent. Why deep learning mitigates dimensionality remains open in 2026. [PMLR paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v336/feldman26a.html)

Replace “many pixels, yet DNNs work” with measurements of **intrinsic/effective dimension, architectural symmetry, locality, data spectra, and sample-to-dimension ratios**.

## 6. Classical classifiers remain strong baselines

SVMs, minimum-distance classifiers, LDA/QDA, and naive Bayes are not universal competitors to large foundation models, but remain important for small datasets, interpretability, probabilistic-assumption checks, calibration, and inexpensive deployment.

- Nearest class mean is Gaussian Bayes with shared identity covariance and equal priors.
- LDA gives Gaussian Bayes boundaries with shared covariance; QDA allows class-specific covariances.
- SVM kernels support convex optimization in fixed feature spaces, but kernel-matrix time and memory become bottlenecks at large sample counts.
- Even when independence fails, naive Bayes offers useful low-variance, fast-learning bias with limited data.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Generalization** | Maintaining low risk on unseen inputs; specify shift/OOD axes as well as i.i.d. evaluation. |
| **Capacity and interpolation** | Generalization depends on data spectra, margins, optimization paths, and implicit regularization, not just parameter count. |
| **Validation leakage** | Repeated use of validation/test results in development effectively fits those evaluation sets. |
| **Calibration** | Agreement between predicted probabilities and observed frequencies, checked by group, OOD condition, and time. |
| **Conformal prediction** | Set-valued predictions with finite-sample coverage under exchangeability; reassess assumptions and weighting under shift. |
| **Intrinsic dimension** | Effective degrees of freedom actually used rather than observed dimension; a candidate explanation for high-dimensional generalization. |
