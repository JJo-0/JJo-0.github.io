A composite index condenses multiple benchmarks into one number. This is useful, but rankings can change when objectives and weights differ. Sources were checked on November 25, 2026; release-time and current versions are distinguished.

## 1. Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v5

The [official AA v5 announcement](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/artificial-analysis-intelligence-index-v5/) was published on October 27, 2026. It assigns 25% each to knowledge work, coding, science and general ability. According to the announcement, 60% of the index is based on evaluations with private test sets or held-out answers.

| Domain | Evaluation | Weight |
|---|---|---:|
| Knowledge Work | AA-Briefcase | 15% |
| Knowledge Work | AutomationBench-AA | 5% |
| Knowledge Work | GDP.pdf | 5% |
| Coding | FrontierCode 2.0 | 15% |
| Coding | Terminal-Bench 4.0 | 10% |
| Science | Terminal-Bench-Science | 15% |
| Science | CritPt v2 Hard | 10% |
| General | AA-Omniscience v1.1 Accuracy | 10% |
| General | AA-Omniscience v1.1 Non-hallucination | 5% |
| General | HLE-Diamond | 10% |

Nine evaluations produce ten weighted entries because Omniscience contributes two metrics. The [current AA site](https://artificialanalysis.ai/) shows v5.0.2 and Terminal-Bench 4.1. Do not treat v5.0 and v5.0.2 as the same version.

## 2. Weighted aggregation and normalization

A conceptual composite index transforms component evaluations to a common scale and then takes a weighted sum.

<Math display tex={"I=\\sum_{j=1}^{m}w_j s_j,\\qquad \\sum_{j=1}^{m}w_j=1"} />

If the normalized scores of four equally weighted domains are 60, 80, 40 and 60, the index is 60. Raising coding by ten points raises the composite by 2.5. This demonstrates weighted aggregation; it is not a reproduction of AA's full implementation, including Elo transformations. Do not directly average raw AA-Briefcase Elo with percentages.

AA is an index covering general capabilities. Dataset selection, versions, difficulty and normalization affect the resulting composite.

## 3. Vals Index v2.2 — industry weights

According to the [official Vals methodology](https://www.vals.ai/benchmarks/vals_index), v2.2 was released on October 19, 2026. It uses approximate US industry GDP shares as weights.

| Sector | Component tests | Original weight | Normalized share |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Finance | Finance Agent v2.1, Excel Modeling v1.1 | 8.0 | 52.29% |
| Coding | Terminal-Bench 4, Vibe Code, Code Migration | 5.6 | 36.60% |
| Legal | Legal Research, HLAB-Vals-1.1 | 1.2 | 7.84% |
| Tax | Tax Agent Bench | 0.5 | 3.27% |

Average the tests within each sector, then combine them as follows.

<Math display tex={"I_{\\mathrm{Vals}}=\\frac{8F+5.6C+1.2L+0.5T}{15.3}"} />

For a hypothetical model with F=60, C=70, L=40 and T=20, the numerator is 480+392+48+10=930 and the index is about 60.78%. Increasing finance by ten percentage points increases the index by about 5.23 points. Increasing tax by ten points raises it by about 0.33 points.

Code Migration in v2.2 uses a fixed subset of the official run; do not equate it to the complete original benchmark. Tax also switched to all-pass accuracy.

## 5. Why weights change rankings

Hypothetical model A scores 70 in finance, 40 in coding, 50 in legal and 50 in tax. Model B scores 50 in finance, 70 in coding, 50 in legal and 50 in tax.

| Model | Simple mean of four domains | Vals weighted mean |
|---|---:|---:|
| A | 52.50% | 56.80% |
| B | 55.00% | 57.32% |

B leads under both aggregations. Increase A's finance score to 72: its simple mean is 53%, still below B, but its Vals weighted mean is about 57.84%, above B. Rankings are therefore a combination of chosen weights and evaluation outcomes, not an absolute ordering of intelligence.

GDP shares are not the share of labor AI can actually replace. Adoption, error costs, regulations, human review, latency and operational costs are also required to estimate economic impact.
